# Deep-research brief 5 — a "barrier theorem" for algebraic no-three-in-line constructions: can anything built from residue classes modulo a prime beat 3/2?

(Русское резюме внизу.  Один вопрос, глубоко.  Ответ — файл с источниками, точными формулировками и оценкой перспектив.)

## What we know (do not re-derive; verify only if you doubt it)
Setting.  A set S ⊂ Z² is *lawful* if no three of its points are collinear.  The no-three-in-line problem asks for the largest lawful
subset of the n×n grid; 2n is an upper bound; the best general lower bound is 3n/2 − o(n) (Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild 1975): for
n = 2p take the modular hyperbola H(c,p) = {xy ≡ c (mod p)} in the box G(p) = [−(p−1)/2, (3p−1)/2] × [0, 2p−1] (each of the p−1
residue classes of H has exactly four lifts in G(p), at the corners of a p×p square) and keep three of the four lifts.
Nothing better than 3/2 is known since 1975 (Green, "100 open problems", Problem 72; Kovács–Nagy–Szabó, arXiv:2508.07632 /
Adv. Comb. 2026:7, and Ghosal–Goenka–Grebennikov–Keevash–Kwan–Pham, arXiv:2607.05255, settle k ≥ 3 points per line asymptotically
(kn) by randomised algebraic constructions and say explicitly that k = 2 is different).

Our results (public repo https://github.com/iwasborninbali/saturation, tag hjsw-note-v0.6, file paper/hjsw_window.pdf):
- Theorem: for every odd prime p, every c ≠ 0 and EVERY 2p×2p box, a lawful subset of H(c,p) ∩ box has at most 3(p−1) points;
  in the HJSW box the number of maximum sets is exactly 9^s (s = number of quadratic residues among c, −c); the LP relaxation
  ("≤ 2 points per rich line") is exact there; the maximum for an arbitrary box is 12n₂+10n₁+8n₀+6s (orbit gadgets).
  Mechanism: a line meets at most two residue classes of the hyperbola (Lagrange), so a rich line contains two congruent
  points, hence has slope 0, ∞, ±1; the rich lines live inside orbits of the Klein group generated by (a,b)↦(−b,−a), (a,b)↦(b,a).
- Exact computations (SAT/MIP/CP-SAT, witnesses verified): unions of TWO hyperbolae H(1,p) ∪ H(k,p) in the same box have maximum
  lawful subsets 35, 41, 54, 59 for p = 11, 13, 17, 19 (best k), and 70 ≤ max ≤ 74 at p = 23: gains over 3(p−1) of +5, +5, +6, +5, ≥+4
  — no growth visible.  Parabola y = x²+k together with H(1): +4, +3, +3, +4.  Three hyperbolae: +7 (p=11), between +7 and +12 (13),
  +8..+15 (17).  Two hyperbolae with DIFFERENT primes p < q in the 2p×2p box: gains +1..+3 for all 11 pairs computed (7 ≤ p < q ≤ 29).
  Larger boxes do not raise the ratio (3p×3p: ≈ 4.3(p−1) points, ratio 1.29–1.35 to the side).
- Strong modular arcs (no three points with det ≡ 0 mod m) cannot beat 3/2 for squarefree m (submultiplicativity,
  Stępień–Szymaszkiewicz 2018 style) — closed.  Local search, warm starts etc. — irrelevant here.
- Heuristic for the pair: fix the HJSW set S₂ of H(1); a point of the second hyperbola is "blocked" if some line through it carries
  two points of S₂; the number of blocking pairs per point grows like log p and only 0–2 points are unblocked for p ≤ 101.

## The question
Is there — or can there be — a THEOREM of the following kind ("barrier theorem"):
  (B) For fixed integers c ≥ 2 and d ≥ 1, let A ⊂ F_p² be the set of F_p-points of an algebraic curve of degree ≤ d (or, more
      generally, any set meeting every line of F_p² in at most t points), and let L(A) ⊂ Z² be its lift into a box of side cp.
      Then every lawful S ⊂ L(A) has |S| ≤ (3/2 + o(1))·cp as p → ∞  (o(1) depending on c, d or t).
Special cases: d = 2 (conics; c = 2 is our theorem, exactly 3(p−1)); unions of two conics (t = 4; our data say +O(1));
c = 3 (our data say ratio 1.35 < 1.5).  Note that (B) MUST fail for t ≈ p (then A can be everything and S any lawful set,
2n points for n ≤ 70 exist), so the dependence on t is essential; a natural sharper form: |S| ≤ 3cp/2 + O(t·p^{1/2}) or similar.

## Questions for the research
1. **Literature on upper bounds for lawful subsets of structured/modular point sets.**  Anything proving that sets built from few
   residue classes per row, lifts of arcs/curves, or "periodic" sets cannot exceed a fraction of the grid in general position?
   Search: "no three in line" + "modular"/"periodic"/"upper bound"/"lift"; general position subsets of lattices; "Erdős
   construction optimal"; Guy–Kelly heuristics for structured sets; papers citing HJSW 1975 (there are few — list them all with
   what they do).  Also the k-points-per-line literature (Kovács–Nagy–Szabó §5 discussion; Ghosal et al.) — quote every sentence
   about why k = 2 resists and about limits of algebraic constructions.
2. **The "copies" argument and its limits.**  HJSW's proof of lawfulness uses that three collinear points in the lift of an arc
   include two congruent points, and that congruent points differ by (ip, jp) with |i|,|j| < c.  Is there a general lemma bounding
   the size of a lawful subset of the lift of a t-arc in a box of side cp in terms of the incidence structure of the "copy
   directions"?  Any use of this in the literature (Roth, Erdős, Kløve, Anderson, Flammenkamp)?  In particular Kløve's and
   Anderson's older bounds for no-three-in-line in the torus / modular grids.
3. **Fractional/LP and Fourier obstructions.**  For one hyperbola the LP with rich-line constraints is tight; for two it is not
   (LP ≈ 47.5 vs 41 at p = 13).  Is there literature on the fractional version (max fractional general-position set), on
   "collinear-triple hypergraph" LP/SDP bounds, or on Fourier-analytic bounds for lawful sets with modular structure (e.g.
   Green–Tao style, or the "no three in a line in F_p^n / Z_N" literature)?  What would a spectral bound for the pair look like?
4. **Counting collinear triples in lifts.**  Known estimates for the number of collinear triples among lifts of a curve mod p into a
   box (incidence bounds, Bombieri–Pila, Vinogradov-type sums)?  We observe ~11·N log N cross triples for two-prime unions.  Is
   there a theorem: "the lift of a t-arc into a cp-box has ≥ f(p) collinear triples", forcing deletions of order p?
5. **What KNS/GGGKKP already know.**  Extract precisely: their upper-bound discussion for k = 2, their remark on why S₂ cannot be
   improved within their framework, and any conjecture that 3/2 is optimal for "algebraic" sets.  Also Green's exact wording of
   Problem 72 and his commentary.
6. **Assessment.**  Given all this: (i) is (B) plausibly TRUE for bounded t?  (ii) is any special case provable with known tools —
   e.g. two conics in the 2p×2p box, or one conic in a 3p×3p box (we have exact data to test conjectures: 27, 43, 52, 69, 77, 93 for
   p = 7…23)?  (iii) what is the smallest statement whose proof would be publishable as a "barrier for algebraic constructions"?
   (iv) who works on this (names, recent arXiv), and would such a result be considered a real contribution or a footnote?

## Резюме по-русски
Ищем «барьерную теорему»: ничего, построенного из классов вычетов по простому p (кривые малой степени, объединения гипербол,
любые множества с ≤ t точками на прямой mod p), поднятого в бокс со стороной cp, не даёт больше (3/2 + o(1))·cp точек без трёх на
прямой.  У нас доказан случай одна гипербола / бокс 2p×2p (точно 3(p−1)) и есть точные данные для пар, троек, парабола+гипербола,
двух простых, боксов 3p×3p — все говорят «+O(1)».  Нужно: что известно о верхних оценках для таких структурированных множеств,
пределы метода «копий» HJSW, LP/Фурье‑препятствия, подсчёт коллинеарных троек в поднятиях, точные слова KNS/Грина о k=2, и
честная оценка: доказуемо ли что‑то из этого известными средствами и будет ли это вкладом.
