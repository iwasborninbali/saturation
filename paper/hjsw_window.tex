\begin{abstract}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $c\in\Fp^*$, and let $\Hc=\{(x,y)\in\Z^2: xy\equiv c \pmod p\}$ be the modular hyperbola.
Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild (1975) observed that the $2p\times2p$ window
$\Gp=[-\tfrac{p-1}{2},\tfrac{3p-1}{2}]\times[0,2p-1]$ contains exactly four points of each of the $p-1$ residue classes of
$\Hc$, at the vertices of a $p\times p$ square, and that keeping three of the four (the twelve outer blocks $T_2$ of the
$4\times4$ block subdivision of $\Gp$) yields $3(p-1)$ points with no three collinear.  Kov\'acs, Nagy and Szab\'o
(2025/26) state that this deletion is optimal, i.e.\ that $\Hc\cap T_2$ is a largest no-three-in-line subset of
$\Hc\cap\Gp$.  We give a short self-contained proof of this statement for every $c$, and we determine the complete
extremal structure: we classify all lines carrying three or more points of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ (they have slope $\pm1$, and
their number is $\tfrac32(p-1)-s$), we exhibit an explicit linear-programming certificate for the bound $3(p-1)$
(so even the fractional relaxation has value $3(p-1)$), and we show that the number of maximum no-three-in-line
subsets is exactly $9^{s}$, where $s\in\{0,1,2\}$ is the number of quadratic residues among $c$ and $-c$.  In
particular the HJSW set is the unique maximum if and only if $p\equiv1\pmod 4$ and $c$ is a quadratic non-residue.
The bound does not depend on the position of the window: for \emph{every} $2p\times2p$ box $[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$
the modular hyperbola contains at most $3(p-1)$ points in general position, and we determine the exact maximum and the
number of maximum sets for every box (an ``orbit lemma'': a box separates $0$ or $2$ of the four partner pairs of each
orbit of the Klein four-group generated by $(a,b)\mapsto(-b,-a)$ and $(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)$, and the maximum is
$12a+10b+8c+6s$ in terms of the numbers of orbits of the three possible types); the HJSW box is among the boxes where
$3(p-1)$ is attained.  The classification also gives the no-four-in-line
analogue: the largest no-four-in-line subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ has exactly $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$ points, so the set $S_3$ of
Kov\'acs--Nagy--Szab\'o is optimal as well.
Everything is verified by independent programs for all primes $p\le101$ (all boxes for $p\le61$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The no-three-in-line problem asks for the largest subset of the $n\times n$ grid with no three collinear points; at most
$2n$ points are possible, and the best general lower bound, $\tfrac32 n-o(n)$, is due to Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild
\cite{HJSW75}: for $n=2p$, $p$ prime, they place $3(p-1)$ points of a modular hyperbola $\Hc$ in the window $\Gp$.  Their
construction rests on two facts: every line meets $\Hc$ in at most two residue classes, and $\Gp$ contains exactly four
points of each class, at the vertices of a $p\times p$ square; keeping the three copies outside the middle block of the
$4\times4$ block subdivision of $\Gp$ leaves no three collinear.

Kov\'acs, Nagy and Szab\'o \cite{KNS25} recently used the same window and its refinements for the no-$(k+1)$-in-line
problem.  In their setting the HJSW set is the case $|W|=1$, $k=2$ of a general randomised construction (all four
copies of every class are taken, then a minimum set $R$ of points is deleted so that every line of slope $\pm1$ retains
at most $k$ points), and they state that for $|W|=1$, $k=2$ ``Subsection 2.2 essentially details an optimal choice for the points $R$ to be
removed to gain the largest no-3-in-line set $S_2(c,p)$ inside $S(W)$'' \cite[App.~A]{KNS25}.
The general estimate of \cite[Rem.~3.4]{KNS25}, $|R|\ge\tfrac12 X$ with $X=\sum_\ell(|\ell\cap S(W)|-k)^+$, gives for
$|W|=1$, $k=2$ the bound $|R|\ge p-1-s$ (see Remark~\ref{rem:kns} below), i.e.\ $|S|\le3(p-1)+s$ for every no-three-in-line
$S\subseteq\Hc\cap\Gp$: exact when neither $c$ nor $-c$ is a square modulo $p$, but one or two short of the truth
otherwise.  We are not aware of a proof of the exact statement in \cite{KNS25}; the purpose of this note is to give one,
and to determine the whole extremal structure of the problem.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Results.}  Throughout, $p$ is an odd prime, $h=(p-1)/2$, $c\in\Fp^*$, and
\[
 \Hc=\{(x,y)\in\Z^2:\ xy\equiv c \pmod p\},\qquad
 \Gp=\{-h,\dots,3h+1\}\times\{0,\dots,2p-1\},
\]
and $\Pset:=\Hc\cap\Gp$.  A set is \emph{lawful} (no-three-in-line, in general position) if it contains no three collinear points.  A line is
\emph{rich} if it contains at least three points of $\Pset$; $\Lc$ denotes the set of rich lines.  Let
$s=[\,c\text{ is a QR}\,]+[\,-c\text{ is a QR}\,]\in\{0,1,2\}$ (QR = quadratic residue modulo $p$).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every rich line has slope $+1$ or $-1$; there are exactly $\tfrac32(p-1)-s$ rich lines, $p-1$ of them
 carrying three points of $\Pset$ and $\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ carrying four (Proposition~\ref{prop:lines} lists them); exactly
 $2s$ points of $\Pset$ lie on no rich line.
\item[(ii)] Every lawful subset of $\Pset$ has at most $3(p-1)$ points; the bound is attained.  Even the fractional
 relaxation (maximise $\sum_q x_q$ subject to $\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le2$ for $\ell\in\Lc$, $0\le x_q\le1$) has optimum
 $3(p-1)$.
\item[(iii)] The number of lawful subsets of size $3(p-1)$ is exactly $9^{s}$.  Every maximum set coincides with the
 HJSW set $\Hc\cap T_2$ outside the (at most four) classes $(a,\pm a)$ with $a^2=\pm c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:qr}
If $p\equiv3\pmod4$ then $s=1$ for every $c$: there are exactly $9$ maximum sets.  If $p\equiv1\pmod4$ then $s=2$ for
$c$ a QR ($81$ maximum sets) and $s=0$ for $c$ a non-residue: the HJSW set is the unique maximum.  Thus $\Hc\cap T_2$
is the unique largest lawful subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ if and only if $p\equiv1\pmod4$ and $c$ is a quadratic non-residue.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:window}
Let $x_0,y_0\in\Z$ and $W=[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$.  Every lawful subset of $\Hc\cap W$ has at most $3(p-1)$
points.  More precisely, the maximum equals $12a+10b+8c+6s$, where $a,b,c$ are the numbers of generic orbits of the
Klein four-group whose partner pairs are split by the box in the patterns $\{(0,2),(2,0)\}$, $(1,1)$, $(0,0)$ respectively
($a+b+c=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$; no other pattern occurs), and the number of maximum sets is $144^{b}\,1296^{c}\,9^{s_1}6^{s_2}$,
where $s_1$ (resp.\ $s_2$) is the number of exceptional orbits whose pair is split (resp.\ shared), $s_1+s_2=s$.  In
particular $3(p-1)$ is attained iff $b=c=0$; the HJSW box has $a=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$, $s_1=s$.
\end{theorem}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a double count over the rich lines: for a lawful $S$, $|S|\le Z+2|\Lc|$ where $Z$ is
the number of points on no rich line, and the identity $Z+2|\Lc|=3(p-1)$ follows from the classification of the rich lines.  The count is
exact precisely because the $2s$ points that lie on no rich line are accounted separately; this is the correction to
the averaging bound of \cite{KNS25} mentioned above.  All rich lines lie inside orbits of a Klein four-group acting on
the classes, and the equality analysis reduces to two small ``gadgets'' (Figure~\ref{fig:gadgets}), which gives $9^s$.
For a general box the rich lines still lie inside the orbits, so the maximum is a sum of orbit maxima; the orbit lemma
(Section~\ref{sec:window}) says that a box splits $0$ or $2$ of the four partner pairs of a generic orbit, and the four
possible patterns have maxima $8$, $10$, $12$, $12$ --- whence the exact formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}.  For boxes
other than the HJSW box the maximum can be smaller than $3(p-1)$ (Table~\ref{tab:shifts}).
The four-point rich lines are pairwise disjoint, which settles the no-four-in-line analogue as well
(Corollary~\ref{cor:no4}: the maximum is $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$ in the HJSW box, attained by the set $S_3$ of \cite{KNS25}).

\section{Notation}

Two points are \emph{congruent} if their coordinates agree modulo $p$; a \emph{class} is a congruence class,
identified with an element of $\Fp\times\Fp$.  Points of $\Hc$ have both coordinates prime to $p$, so the classes of
$\Hc$ are the $p-1$ pairs $\kappa=(a,b)\in\Fp^*\times\Fp^*$ with $ab=c$.  Since each coordinate range of $\Gp$ is an
interval of $2p$ consecutive integers, it contains exactly two representatives of every residue class; hence
every class $\kappa$ of $\Hc$ has exactly four points in $\Gp$ and $|\Pset|=4(p-1)$.  We write
$(x_\kappa,y_\kappa)$ for the \emph{base copy} of $\kappa$, its unique point in
$\{-h,\dots,h\}\times\{1,\dots,p-1\}$ (necessarily $x_\kappa\ne0$), and
\[
 \kappa(r,s):=(x_\kappa+rp,\;y_\kappa+sp),\qquad r,s\in\{0,1\},
\]
for its four points in $\Gp$.  Following \cite{HJSW75,KNS25} we sort the classes into four \emph{types} according
to the position of the base copy:
\[
 A:\ x_\kappa>0,\ y_\kappa>h;\qquad B:\ x_\kappa<0,\ y_\kappa>h;\qquad C:\ x_\kappa>0,\ y_\kappa\le h;\qquad
 D:\ x_\kappa<0,\ y_\kappa\le h .
\]
(In the notation of \cite[Def.~2.3]{KNS25}: $\kappa(0,0)\in A_{0,0}$, $B_{-1,0}$, $C_{0,0}$, $D_{-1,0}$ respectively; the
HJSW set $T_2$ omits, for each type, the copy $A_{0,0}$, $B_{0,0}$, $C_{0,1}$, $D_{0,1}$, i.e.\ $\kappa(0,0)$ for
$A$, $\kappa(1,0)$ for $B$, $\kappa(0,1)$ for $C$, $\kappa(1,1)$ for $D$; the union of these omitted blocks is the middle
block $M$ of \cite[\S2.3]{KNS25}.  We call this copy the \emph{middle copy} of $\kappa$.)  We write $n_A,n_B,n_C,n_D$
for the numbers of classes of each type, so $n_A+n_B+n_C+n_D=p-1$.  Finally, for a class $\kappa$ put
\[
 d_\kappa:=x_\kappa-y_\kappa,\qquad e_\kappa:=x_\kappa+y_\kappa,\qquad
 D_\kappa:=\{x-y=d_\kappa\},\qquad E_\kappa:=\{x+y=e_\kappa+p\};
\]
$D_\kappa$ (the \emph{diagonal} of $\kappa$) contains $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ and $E_\kappa$ (the \emph{antidiagonal})
contains $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$.  For a real number $x$ let $x^+=\max\{x,0\}$.

Three involutions act on the classes of $\Hc$:
\[
 \sigma(a,b)=(-b,-a),\qquad \tau(a,b)=(b,a),\qquad \nu(a,b)=(-a,-b)=\sigma\tau(a,b)=\tau\sigma(a,b),
\]
so $V=\{1,\sigma,\tau,\nu\}$ is a Klein four-group acting on the $p-1$ classes.  $\sigma$ has fixed classes iff $-c$ is a
QR (then exactly two: $(a,-a)$ with $a^2=-c$), $\tau$ has fixed classes iff $c$ is a QR (two: $(a,a)$ with $a^2=c$),
and $\nu$ has none.  Put $f_\sigma:=\#\{\kappa:\sigma\kappa=\kappa\}\in\{0,2\}$, $f_\tau:=\#\{\kappa:\tau\kappa=\kappa\}\in\{0,2\}$;
thus $f_\sigma+f_\tau=2s$.  The $V$-orbits are of size~4 (\emph{generic}: four distinct classes) or of size~2
(\emph{exceptional}: $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ with $\kappa$ and $\nu\kappa$ both fixed by $\sigma$, or both fixed by $\tau$);
there are exactly $s$ exceptional orbits.

\section{Rich lines}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lagrange}
Let $\ell$ be a line containing at least two points of $\Hc$.  Then the points of $\Hc\cap\ell$ lie in at most two
classes.  More precisely, if $\ell$ contains a point of class $\kappa$, then the classes met by $\ell$ are:
only $\kappa$ if $\ell$ has slope $0$ or $\infty$; among $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ if $\ell$ has slope $+1$;
among $\{\kappa,\tau\kappa\}$ if $\ell$ has slope $-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(u,v)\in\Z^2$ be a primitive direction vector of $\ell$ and $(x_0,y_0)\in\Hc\cap\ell$.  The lattice points of
$\ell$ are $(x_0+tu,y_0+tv)$, $t\in\Z$, and such a point lies on $\Hc$ iff
$uv\,t^2+(x_0v+y_0u)\,t\equiv0\pmod p$ (we used $x_0y_0\equiv c$).  The class of the point depends only on
$t\bmod p$.  If $uv\not\equiv0$, the congruence has at most two roots modulo $p$; if exactly one of $u,v$ is
divisible by $p$ (both cannot be, as $\gcd(u,v)=1$), it reduces to $x_0v\,t\equiv0$ or $y_0u\,t\equiv0$ with a unit
coefficient, so $t\equiv0$: one class.  For $(u,v)=(1,0),(0,1)$ this gives the claim for slopes $0,\infty$.
For $(u,v)=(1,1)$ the roots are $t\equiv0$ and $t\equiv-(x_0+y_0)$, the latter giving the point
$(-y_0,-x_0)$ modulo $p$, of class $\sigma\kappa$.  For $(u,v)=(1,-1)$ the roots are $t\equiv0$ and
$t\equiv y_0-x_0$, giving $(y_0,x_0)$, of class $\tau\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:copies}
Two distinct points of one class in $\Gp$ differ by $\pm(p,0)$, $\pm(0,p)$, $\pm(p,p)$ or $\pm(p,-p)$; the line
through them has slope $0,\infty,+1$ or $-1$ and contains no third point of that class.  The point $\kappa(r,s)$ lies
on the slope-$(+1)$ line $x-y=d_\kappa+(r-s)p$ and on the slope-$(-1)$ line $x+y=e_\kappa+(r+s)p$.  In particular
$D_\kappa$ and $E_\kappa$ are the only lines of slope $\pm1$ carrying two points of $\kappa$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four points of a class are the vertices of an axis-parallel square of side $p$; no three are collinear.
The rest is a direct computation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:slopes}
A rich line has slope $+1$ or $-1$.  Consequently a set $S\subseteq\Pset$ is lawful iff $|S\cap\ell|\le2$ for every
$\ell\in\Lc$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange} three points of $\Pset$ on a line lie in at most two classes, so two of them are congruent,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:copies} the line has slope $0,\infty,\pm1$.  A line of slope $0$ or $\infty$ meets a single class
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), which has at most two points on it (Lemma~\ref{lem:copies}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[position of the partner classes]\label{lem:partners}
For every class $\kappa$ the types of $\sigma\kappa$, $\tau\kappa$ and the shifts $d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa$,
$e_{\tau\kappa}-e_\kappa$ are given by the following table:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cc|cc}
\toprule
type of $\kappa$ & type of $\sigma\kappa$ & $d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa$ & type of $\tau\kappa$ & $e_{\tau\kappa}-e_\kappa$\\
\midrule
$A$ & $A$ & $0$ & $D$ & $-p$\\
$B$ & $C$ & $+p$ & $B$ & $0$\\
$C$ & $B$ & $-p$ & $C$ & $0$\\
$D$ & $D$ & $0$ & $A$ & $+p$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In particular $\sigma$ preserves the types $A$, $D$ and interchanges $B$, $C$; $\tau$ preserves $B$, $C$ and
interchanges $A$, $D$; $\nu$ interchanges $A\leftrightarrow D$ and $B\leftrightarrow C$; the fixed classes of $\sigma$
lie in $A\cup D$, those of $\tau$ in $B\cup C$; and $n_A=n_D$, $n_B=n_C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\kappa=(a,b)$ with base copy $(x_\kappa,y_\kappa)$, so $x_\kappa\equiv a$, $y_\kappa\equiv b$.  The base copy of
$\sigma\kappa=(-b,-a)$ has first coordinate $-y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa\le h$ and $p-y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa>h$, and second
coordinate $p-x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa>0$ and $-x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa<0$ (these are the representatives of $-b$ in
$[-h,h]$ and of $-a$ in $[1,p-1]$).  Going through the four types:
$\kappa\in A$: $(p-y_\kappa,\,p-x_\kappa)$, again of type $A$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in D$: $(-y_\kappa,\,-x_\kappa)$, type $D$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in C$: $(-y_\kappa,\,p-x_\kappa)$, type $B$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa-p$;
$\kappa\in B$: $(p-y_\kappa,\,-x_\kappa)$, type $C$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa+p$.
Similarly, the base copy of $\tau\kappa=(b,a)$ has first coordinate $y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa\le h$ and $y_\kappa-p$
otherwise, and second coordinate $x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa>0$ and $x_\kappa+p$ otherwise:
$\kappa\in B$: $(y_\kappa-p,\,x_\kappa+p)$, type $B$, sum $e_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in C$: $(y_\kappa,\,x_\kappa)$, type $C$, sum $e_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in A$: $(y_\kappa-p,\,x_\kappa)$, type $D$, sum $e_\kappa-p$;
$\kappa\in D$: $(y_\kappa,\,x_\kappa+p)$, type $A$, sum $e_\kappa+p$.
The remaining assertions follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lines}
$\Lc$ consists of the following lines, and no others; for each we list its points in $\Pset$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For $\kappa\in A\cup D$ with $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$: the common diagonal $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$, with the
 four points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$.
\item[(b)] For $\kappa\in B\cup C$ with $\tau\kappa\ne\kappa$: the common antidiagonal $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, with the
 four points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(0,1),\tau\kappa(1,0)$.
\item[(c)] For every $\kappa\in C$: $D_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(1,0)$
 ($\sigma\kappa\in B$); for every $\kappa\in B$: $D_\kappa$ with the three points
 $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,1)$ ($\sigma\kappa\in C$).
\item[(d)] For every $\kappa\in A$: $E_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(1,1)$
 ($\tau\kappa\in D$); for every $\kappa\in D$: $E_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(0,0)$
 ($\tau\kappa\in A$).
\end{enumerate}
The lines listed in (a)--(d) are pairwise distinct (apart from the identifications $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ in (a) and
$E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$ in (b) that are built into the list), and each of them lies inside a single $V$-orbit
of classes.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 |\Lc|=\frac{n_A+n_D-f_\sigma}{2}+\frac{n_B+n_C-f_\tau}{2}+(n_A+n_D)+(n_B+n_C)
 =\frac32(p-1)-s,
\end{equation}
with $p-1$ three-point lines and $\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ four-point lines.  Moreover, the points of $\Pset$ lying on
\emph{no} rich line are exactly $\kappa(1,1)$ for $\sigma$-fixed $\kappa\in A$, $\kappa(0,0)$ for $\sigma$-fixed
$\kappa\in D$, $\kappa(0,1)$ for $\tau$-fixed $\kappa\in B$ and $\kappa(1,0)$ for $\tau$-fixed $\kappa\in C$; their number is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
 Z:=\#\{q\in\Pset:\ q\text{ lies on no rich line}\}=f_\sigma+f_\tau=2s .
\end{equation}
The points lying on \emph{two} rich lines are exactly the middle copies of the classes $\kappa$ whose partner
($\sigma\kappa$ for $\kappa\in A\cup D$, $\tau\kappa$ for $\kappa\in B\cup C$) is different from $\kappa$; no point lies on three
rich lines.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\ell\in\Lc$.  By Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} its slope is $\pm1$; say $+1$ (the case $-1$ is symmetric, with
$\tau$, $e_\kappa$, $E_\kappa$ in place of $\sigma$, $d_\kappa$, $D_\kappa$).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange} the points of
$\ell\cap\Pset$ lie in two classes $\kappa,\sigma\kappa$ (a single class carries at most two points on a line), and
one of the classes, say $\kappa$, has two points on $\ell$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:copies} these are $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$
and $\ell=D_\kappa$, and $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$.  The points of $\sigma\kappa$ on $D_\kappa$ are the
$\sigma\kappa(r,s)$ with $d_{\sigma\kappa}+(r-s)p=d_\kappa$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}: for $\kappa\in A\cup D$,
$r=s$, giving (a); for $\kappa\in C$, $r-s=1$, giving $\sigma\kappa(1,0)$; for $\kappa\in B$, $r-s=-1$, giving
$\sigma\kappa(0,1)$; this is (c).  Conversely each listed line has at least three points.

Distinctness: if $D_\kappa=D_\lambda$ then this line of slope $+1$ meets both classes $\kappa,\lambda$, so
$\lambda\in\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}; and $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ holds for $\kappa\in A\cup D$
(shift $0$) but not for $\kappa\in B\cup C$ (shift $\pm p$).  Likewise $E_\kappa=E_\lambda$ forces
$\lambda\in\{\kappa,\tau\kappa\}$, and $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$ holds exactly for $\kappa\in B\cup C$.  A rich line meets
only the classes $\kappa,\sigma\kappa$ (or $\kappa,\tau\kappa$), which lie in one $V$-orbit.  Counting: (a) gives
$(n_A+n_D-f_\sigma)/2$ lines, (b) $(n_B+n_C-f_\tau)/2$, (c) $n_B+n_C$, (d) $n_A+n_D$, which is \eqref{eq:L}.

For the incidences of a single point, take $\kappa\in A$.  Its points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$ lie on $E_\kappa\in\Lc$
(d), and on no line of type (a)/(c) (their slope-$(+1)$ lines $x-y=d_\kappa\pm p$ carry no second point of
$\kappa$ and, by the table, no point of $\sigma\kappa$).  $\kappa(1,1)$ lies on $D_\kappa$, which is rich iff
$\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$, and on $x+y=e_\kappa+2p=e_{\tau\kappa}+3p$, which carries no point of $\tau\kappa$: so
$\kappa(1,1)$ is on one rich line if $\sigma\kappa\neq\kappa$ and on none otherwise.  $\kappa(0,0)$ lies on
$D_\kappa$ and on $x+y=e_\kappa=e_{\tau\kappa}+p$, i.e.\ on $E_{\tau\kappa}\in\Lc$: two rich lines if $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$,
one otherwise.  The types $D$, $B$, $C$ are analogous (for $B$: $\kappa(1,0)\in D_{\sigma\kappa}$ since
$d_\kappa+p=d_{\sigma\kappa}$, while $\kappa(0,1)$ lies on $x-y=d_\kappa-p=d_{\sigma\kappa}-2p$, which carries no point of
$\sigma\kappa$; for $C$ the roles of $(1,0),(0,1)$ are exchanged; for $D$: $\kappa(1,1)\in E_{\tau\kappa}$ since
$e_\kappa+2p=e_{\tau\kappa}+p$, and $\kappa(0,0)$ lies on $x+y=e_\kappa=e_{\tau\kappa}-p$).  This gives \eqref{eq:Z}
and the last two assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:qr}}\label{sec:proof}

Part (i) is Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} and Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Upper bound.}
Let $S\subseteq\Pset$ be lawful.  For $q\in\Pset$ let $m(q)$ be the number of rich lines through $q$.  Every rich line
carries at most two points of $S$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}), hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
\begin{aligned}
 |S| \;&=\; \#\{q\in S: m(q)=0\}+\#\{q\in S: m(q)\ge1\}
 \;\le\; Z+\sum_{q\in S}m(q)-\sum_{q\in S}\bigl(m(q)-1\bigr)^{+}\\
 &=\; Z+\sum_{\ell\in\Lc}|S\cap\ell|-\sum_{q\in S}\bigl(m(q)-1\bigr)^{+}
 \;\le\; Z+2|\Lc| ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and by \eqref{eq:L}, \eqref{eq:Z}, $Z+2|\Lc|=2s+3(p-1)-2s=3(p-1)$.  The HJSW set attains the bound \cite{HJSW75}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{LP certificate.}  The same count is valid for fractional $x\in[0,1]^{\Pset}$ with
$\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le2$ ($\ell\in\Lc$): $\sum_q x_q\le\sum_{q:\,m(q)=0}x_q+\sum_{\ell\in\Lc}\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le Z+2|\Lc|$.
In LP-duality terms the dual solution assigns weight $1$ to every rich line and weight $1$ to the bound $x_q\le1$ of each
of the $Z$ points on no rich line.  This proves (ii).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Equality.}  $|S|=3(p-1)$ holds iff all inequalities in \eqref{eq:count} are equalities, i.e.\ iff
(I) $S$ contains all $Z$ points lying on no rich line; (II) $|S\cap\ell|=2$ for every $\ell\in\Lc$; (III) no point of $S$
lies on two rich lines.  Since every rich line lies inside one $V$-orbit of classes (Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}),
conditions (I)--(III) split into independent conditions on the orbits, and the number of maximum sets is the product of
the numbers of admissible choices in the orbits.  In a generic orbit $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa,\tau\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ all four
classes have distinct partners; the six rich lines meeting the orbit (two of type (a) or (b), four of type (c) or (d))
meet no other orbit, its four middle copies have $m=2$ and are excluded by (III), and each of the six lines then retains
exactly two points, both with $m=1$, which are forced into $S$ by (II): on a generic orbit $S$ coincides with
$\Hc\cap T_2$, and there is exactly one admissible choice.  An exceptional orbit $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ ($\kappa$ and
$\nu\kappa$ fixed by $\sigma$, or both fixed by $\tau$) meets exactly two rich lines, each with three points of $m=1$,
and contains two points with $m=0$: conditions (I)--(III) allow $\binom32\binom32=9$ choices, and all of them are lawful
by Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}.  There are $s$ exceptional orbits, whence $9^{s}$ maximum sets, all agreeing with the
HJSW set outside the exceptional orbits, i.e.\ outside the classes $(a,\pm a)$ with $a^2=\pm c$.  This proves (iii).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Corollary~\ref{cor:qr}.}  $-1$ is a QR modulo $p$ iff $p\equiv1\pmod4$.  If $p\equiv3\pmod4$,
exactly one of $c,-c$ is a QR, so $s=1$; if $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $c$ and $-c$ have the same character, so $s\in\{0,2\}$
according as $c$ is a non-residue or a residue.  \qed

\begin{remark}\label{rem:kns}
(a) In the language of \cite[Constr.~3.2, Rem.~3.4]{KNS25} with $W=\{c\}$, $k=2$ (so $S(W)=\Pset$ and $L'$ is the set of
lines of slope $\pm1$): $X_{2,p}(\{c\})=\sum_{\ell}(|\ell\cap\Pset|-2)^+=(p-1)\cdot1+(\tfrac12(p-1)-s)\cdot2=2(p-1)-2s$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i), so the general bound $|R|\ge\tfrac12X$ gives $|R|\ge p-1-s$ and hence, for every lawful
$S\subseteq\Pset$, $|S|\le|\Pset|-|R|\le3(p-1)+s$ (a lawful $S$ is a trimming, by Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}).  The
averaging loses exactly one point per exceptional orbit, because each such orbit contains two points on no rich line
and no point on two rich lines; the count \eqref{eq:count} keeps track of these points and is exact.

(b) The theorem says that no lawful set can use the middle block $M$ profitably: adding fourth copies to the HJSW
set is impossible without removing at least as many points.  Any improvement of the HJSW construction must therefore
use points outside $\Hc\cap\Gp$ (another hyperbola, a larger window --- by Theorem~\ref{thm:window} not merely a
shifted $2p\times2p$ box --- or non-hyperbola points).

(c) The proof uses only: at most two classes per line (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), the square arrangement of the four
copies, and the position of the partner classes (Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}).  It does not use $p\ge7$; $p=3$ and $p=5$
are covered as well.
\end{remark}

\section{All $2p\times2p$ boxes}\label{sec:window}

Let $W=[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$ with $x_0,y_0\in\Z$ (only $x_0,y_0$ modulo $p$ matter), $\Pset_W=\Hc\cap W$, and let
$\rho_x(u)\in[x_0,x_0+p)$, $\rho_y(u)\in[y_0,y_0+p)$ denote the representatives of a residue $u$.  Every class $\kappa=(a,b)$
of $\Hc$ again has exactly four points $\kappa(r,s)=(\rho_x(a)+rp,\rho_y(b)+sp)$, $r,s\in\{0,1\}$, in $W$; put
$d_\kappa=\rho_x(a)-\rho_y(b)$, $e_\kappa=\rho_x(a)+\rho_y(b)$, $D_\kappa=\{x-y=d_\kappa\}$, $E_\kappa=\{x+y=e_\kappa+p\}$.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:lagrange}, \ref{lem:copies} and Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} hold verbatim (they do not use the position of
the box), so a rich line is $D_\kappa$ for a class with $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$ or $E_\kappa$ for a class with $\tau\kappa\ne\kappa$,
and its points are the two copies $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ (resp.\ $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$) together with the copies of the
partner class on the same line.  Since $d_\kappa\equiv d_{\sigma\kappa}\pmod p$ and both lie in an interval of length $2p-2$,
$d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa\in\{-p,0,p\}$; we call the $\sigma$-pair $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ \emph{shared} if
$d_{\sigma\kappa}=d_\kappa$ (then $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ carries the four points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),
\sigma\kappa(1,1)$) and \emph{split} otherwise (then $D_\kappa$ carries $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ and one copy of $\sigma\kappa$,
and $D_{\sigma\kappa}$ carries $\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$ and one copy of $\kappa$).  Likewise a $\tau$-pair is
shared ($E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, four points) or split (two lines with three points).  In the HJSW box the $\sigma$-pairs in
$A\cup D$ and the $\tau$-pairs in $B\cup C$ are shared and the others are split (Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}); in the box
$[0,2p)^2$ all pairs are shared.

\begin{lemma}[orbit lemma]\label{lem:orbit}
Let $O=\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa,\tau\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ be a generic orbit, and let $e_O$ (resp.\ $f_O$) be the number of split
$\sigma$-pairs (resp.\ $\tau$-pairs) among the two $\sigma$-pairs and two $\tau$-pairs of $O$.  Then $e_O+f_O\in\{0,2\}$; in
particular $e_O+f_O\le2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\kappa=(a,b)$, $X_u=\rho_x(u)$, $X'_u=\rho_x(-u)$, $Y_u=\rho_y(u)$, $Y'_u=\rho_y(-u)$ for $u\in\{a,b\}$.  The base copies
are $P=(X_a,Y_b)$ of $\kappa$, $Q=(X'_b,Y'_a)$ of $\sigma\kappa$, $R=(X_b,Y_a)$ of $\tau\kappa$ and $T=(X'_a,Y'_b)$ of $\nu\kappa$;
the $\sigma$-pairs are $\{P,Q\}$, $\{R,T\}$ and the $\tau$-pairs $\{P,R\}$, $\{Q,T\}$.  With $d(x,y)=x-y$, $e(x,y)=x+y$, the four
split conditions are $E_1: d_P\ne d_Q$, $E_2: d_T\ne d_R$, $F_1: e_P\ne e_R$, $F_2: e_Q\ne e_T$; each of the four differences is
$\equiv0\pmod p$ and lies in $[-p,p]$, hence is one of $-p,0,p$.

Let $m_x$ be the least multiple of $p$ exceeding $2x_0$ and $\mu_x=m_x-2x_0\in[1,p]$; similarly $m_y$, $\mu_y$.  Since
$X_u+X'_u\equiv0\pmod p$ and $X_u+X'_u\in[2x_0+1,2x_0+2p-3]$, we have $X_u+X'_u=m_x+\varepsilon_x(u)p$ with
$\varepsilon_x(u)\in\{0,1\}$, and explicitly $\varepsilon_x(u)=[X_u-x_0>\mu_x]$; likewise $Y_u+Y'_u=m_y+\varepsilon_y(u)p$,
$\varepsilon_y(u)=[Y_u-y_0>\mu_y]$.  Put $\Delta=\varepsilon_x(a)-\varepsilon_x(b)$, $\Gamma=\varepsilon_y(a)-\varepsilon_y(b)$,
$A=\varepsilon_x(b)-\varepsilon_y(a)$, $B=\varepsilon_x(a)-\varepsilon_y(b)$, and define the integers $\theta,\eta$ by
\[
 (X_a+X_b)-(Y_a+Y_b)-(m_x-m_y)=\theta p,\qquad (X_b-X_a)-(Y_b-Y_a)=\eta p .
\]
Substituting $X'_u=m_x+\varepsilon_x(u)p-X_u$, $Y'_u=m_y+\varepsilon_y(u)p-Y_u$ one finds
\[
 d_P-d_Q=(\theta-A)p,\quad d_T-d_R=(B-\theta)p,\quad e_R-e_P=\eta p,\quad e_Q-e_T=(\Gamma-\Delta-\eta)p,
\]
so that $E_1\Leftrightarrow\theta\ne A$, $E_2\Leftrightarrow\theta\ne B$, $F_1\Leftrightarrow\eta\ne0$,
$F_2\Leftrightarrow\eta\ne\Gamma-\Delta$, and $\theta-A,\ \theta-B,\ \eta,\ \eta-(\Gamma-\Delta)\in\{-1,0,1\}$.

\emph{Parity.}  For $x\in\{-1,0,1\}$, $[x\ne0]\equiv x\pmod2$.  Hence
$E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\equiv(\theta-A)+(\theta-B)+\eta+(\eta-\Gamma+\Delta)\equiv A+B+\Gamma-\Delta=2\varepsilon_x(b)-2\varepsilon_y(b)
\equiv0\pmod2$: the number of split pairs in $O$ is even.

\emph{If $F_1$ holds, then $E_1$ and $E_2$ do not both hold.}  Put $s=X_a-x_0$, $t=X_b-x_0\in[0,p-1]$ and $v=(x_0-y_0)\bmod p$;
since $Y_u\equiv X_u$, we have $Y_a-y_0=s+v-c_ap$ and $Y_b-y_0=t+v-c_bp$ with $c_a=[s+v\ge p]$, $c_b=[t+v\ge p]$, and therefore
$\eta=c_b-c_a$.  So $F_1$ means $c_a\ne c_b$; the substitution $a\leftrightarrow b$ exchanges $E_1\leftrightarrow E_2$ and
fixes $F_1$, so we may assume $c_a=0$, $c_b=1$, i.e.\ $s\le p-1-v<t$.  Since $\mu_x-\mu_y\equiv-2v\pmod p$ and
$|\mu_x-\mu_y|\le p-1$, we have $\mu_x-\mu_y=-2v+kp$ with $k\in\{0,1,2\}$, and
$\theta p=(2x_0+s+t)-(2y_0+s+t+2v-p)-(\mu_x+2x_0-\mu_y-2y_0)=p-2v-(\mu_x-\mu_y)=(1-k)p$, i.e.\ $\theta=1-k$.
With $\varepsilon_x(a)=[s>\mu_x]$, $\varepsilon_x(b)=[t>\mu_x]$, $\varepsilon_y(a)=[s+v>\mu_y]$, $\varepsilon_y(b)=[t+v-p>\mu_y]$
and $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v-kp$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$k=1$:] $E_1\Leftrightarrow[t>\mu_x]\ne[s>\mu_x+v-p]$ and $E_2\Leftrightarrow[s>\mu_x]\ne[t>\mu_x+v]$.  If $s>\mu_x$
 then $t>\mu_x$ and $s>\mu_x+v-p$, so $E_1$ fails.  If $s\le\mu_x$, $E_2$ forces $t>\mu_x+v$, so $\mu_x+v\le p-2$ and
 $[t>\mu_x]=1$, and $E_1$ forces $s\le\mu_x+v-p<0$, impossible.
\item[$k=0$:] here $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v\le p$, so $\varepsilon_y(b)=0$ and $E_2\Leftrightarrow s\le\mu_x$; then $\varepsilon_y(a)=[s>\mu_x+v]=0$
 and $E_1\Leftrightarrow t\le\mu_x$, but $t\ge p-v>p-2v\ge\mu_x$.
\item[$k=2$:] here $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v-2p\ge1$, so $\mu_x\ge2p+1-2v$ and $\varepsilon_y(a)=1$; $E_1\Leftrightarrow t>\mu_x$ and
 $E_2\Leftrightarrow(s>\mu_x)\vee(t\le\mu_x+v-p)$.  Now $s>\mu_x$ would give $2p+2-2v\le s\le p-1-v$, i.e.\ $v\ge p+3$; so
 $s\le\mu_x$ and $E_2$ needs $t\le\mu_x+v-p<\mu_x$, contradicting $E_1$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, if $F_1$ holds then $E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\le3$, and if $F_1$ fails then again $E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\le3$; by parity the
sum is $0$ or $2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[gadget maxima]\label{lem:gadget}
Let $O$ be an orbit and $\Pset_O\subseteq\Pset_W$ its $16$ (or $8$) points.  Every rich line meeting $\Pset_O$ lies in
$\Pset_O$, and the largest lawful subset of $\Pset_O$ has size and multiplicity as follows: exceptional orbit: $6$ points, $9$
sets if the pair is split and $6$ if it is shared; generic orbit with $(e_O,f_O)=(0,0)$: $8$ points, $6^4=1296$ sets;
$(1,1)$: $10$ points, $144$ sets; $(0,2)$ or $(2,0)$: $12$ points, one set.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A rich line meets only a class and its $\sigma$- or $\tau$-partner (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), hence lies in one orbit.
\emph{Exceptional orbit} $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$, say $\sigma\kappa=\kappa$: the rich lines are $E_\kappa=E_{\nu\kappa}$ (four points)
if the $\tau$-pair is shared, resp.\ $E_\kappa$, $E_{\nu\kappa}$ (three points each) if it is split; the remaining four
(resp.\ two) points are free: $2+4=6$ points in $\binom42=6$ ways, resp.\ $2+2+2=6$ points in $3\cdot3=9$ ways.
\emph{Pattern $(0,0)$}: the two shared diagonals and the two shared antidiagonals are four disjoint four-point lines covering
$\Pset_O$: $4\cdot2=8$ points in $6^4$ ways.
\emph{Pattern $(2,0)$} (and symmetrically $(0,2)$): the four diagonals $D_\lambda$ ($\lambda\in O$) are rich with three points
each and the two antidiagonals $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, $E_{\sigma\kappa}=E_{\nu\kappa}$ have four points; every class $\lambda$ has
exactly one point of multiplicity $m=2$, namely its off-diagonal copy lying on $D_{\sigma\lambda}$ (which is also on the
antidiagonal of $\lambda$).  Each $D_\lambda$ carries $\lambda(0,0),\lambda(1,1)$ ($m=1$) and one $m=2$ point, each antidiagonal
carries two $m=1$ and two $m=2$ points.  By \eqref{eq:count} restricted to $O$ (with $Z=0$), $|S\cap\Pset_O|\le12$ with equality
iff $S$ avoids the four $m=2$ points and takes both remaining points of each of the six lines --- which is lawful; so the
maximum is $12$ and it is unique.  (For the HJSW box this is the generic orbit of Section~\ref{sec:proof}.)
\emph{Pattern $(1,1)$}: the split $\sigma$-pair and the split $\tau$-pair share exactly one class $\kappa$ (any $\sigma$-edge and
any $\tau$-edge of the four-cycle $\kappa\,\text{--}\,\tau\kappa\,\text{--}\,\nu\kappa\,\text{--}\,\sigma\kappa\,\text{--}\,\kappa$
meet in one vertex); the rich lines are $D_\kappa$, $D_{\sigma\kappa}$ (three points), $D_{\tau\kappa}=D_{\nu\kappa}$ (four),
$E_\kappa$, $E_{\tau\kappa}$ (three), $E_{\sigma\kappa}=E_{\nu\kappa}$ (four), and the $m=2$ points are: $\kappa_b\in D_\kappa\cap E_{\tau\kappa}$
and $\kappa_y\in E_\kappa\cap D_{\sigma\kappa}$ (two copies of $\kappa$), $\sigma\kappa_c\in D_\kappa\cap E_{\sigma\kappa}$ and
$\tau\kappa_z\in E_\kappa\cap D_{\tau\kappa}$.  Thus $D_\kappa$ and $E_\kappa$ each contain exactly one point of multiplicity one, and
$D_\kappa\cap E_\kappa=\emptyset$ (a common lattice point would have $x=x_\kappa+p/2$).  For lawful $S$ let $t$ be the number of
$m=2$ points in $S$; then $|S\cap\Pset_O|=\sum_{\ell}|S\cap\ell|-t$ over the six lines, and $|S\cap D_\kappa|=2$ or
$|S\cap E_\kappa|=2$ requires an $m=2$ point of $S$ on that line.  If $t=0$, both $D_\kappa$, $E_\kappa$ carry at most one point:
$|S\cap\Pset_O|\le10$; if $t=1$, at most one of them is full: $\le11-1=10$; if $t\ge2$: $\le12-t\le10$.  The value $10$ is
attained (e.g.\ take $\kappa_b,\kappa_y$, the two other copies of $\kappa$, and two further points on each of the four remaining
lines avoiding $\sigma\kappa_c,\tau\kappa_z$), and a direct enumeration of the ten-point lawful sets in this configuration gives
$9$ with $t=0$, $54$ with $t=1$ and $81$ with $t=2$, i.e.\ $144$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gadget} the rich lines of $\Pset_W$ lie inside the orbits, so a set is lawful iff its intersection with
every orbit is lawful, and the maximum and the number of maximum sets are the sum, resp.\ the product, of the orbit values.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit} every generic orbit has pattern $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,2)$ or $(2,0)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gadget}
gives the formula.  Since $a+b+c=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$, the maximum is $12a+10b+8c+6s\le12(a+b+c)+6s=3(p-1)$, with equality iff
$b=c=0$.  For the HJSW box, Lemma~\ref{lem:partners} gives $(e,f)=(0,2)$ for the $A/D$-orbits and $(2,0)$ for the
$B/C$-orbits, and the exceptional pairs are split; this recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii),(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The double count of Section~\ref{sec:proof} gives, for every box, $|S|\le Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)=12a+12b+8c+6s$
(one checks $Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+\#\{m=2\}$ and $\#\{m=2\}=2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)$ exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:proof}); it is
off by exactly $2$ on every $(1,1)$-orbit, where two rich three-point lines consist of one point of multiplicity one and two
of multiplicity two.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[no-four-in-line]\label{cor:no4}
In every box $W$ the four-point rich lines are pairwise disjoint; hence the largest subset of $\Pset_W$ with no four
collinear points has exactly $4(p-1)-N_{sh}(W)$ points, where $N_{sh}(W)$ is the number of shared pairs, and there are
$4^{N_{sh}(W)}$ such subsets.  By the orbit lemma $N_{sh}(W)\ge2\cdot\#\{\text{generic orbits}\}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$, so the
maximum is at most $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$; in the HJSW box $N_{sh}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ and the set $S_3=\Hc\cap T_3$ of
\cite[Constr.~2.10]{KNS25} (with $|S_3|=\tfrac72(p-1)+s$) is a largest no-four-in-line subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$, as asserted in
\cite[App.~A]{KNS25}.  (No line carries five points of $\Pset_W$, so the no-five-in-line maximum is $|\Pset_W|=4(p-1)$.)
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A four-point rich line is $D_\kappa$ for a shared $\sigma$-pair (points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$)
or $E_\kappa$ for a shared $\tau$-pair (points with $r+s=1$).  The slope-$(-1)$ line through a point $\kappa(r,r)$ is not of
the second kind: if it were $E_\lambda$ then $\kappa\in\{\lambda,\tau\lambda\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}, so $E_\lambda=E_\kappa$,
which contains only the copies $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$ of $\kappa$; likewise the slope-$(+1)$ line through a point
$\kappa(r,1-r)$ is not of the first kind.  Hence the four-point lines are pairwise disjoint, a
no-four-in-line subset must miss at least one point of each of them, and deleting exactly one point from each is
sufficient (all other lines carry at most three points).  For the HJSW box, $N_{sh}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}, and $|S_3|=|S_2|+|M\cap E\cap\Hc|=3(p-1)+\tfrac12(p-1)+s$ because the classes fixed by $\sigma$ or
$\tau$ lie in $E$ and the remaining classes split evenly between $E$ and $F$ \cite[Obs.~2.9]{KNS25}.
\end{proof}

For the box $[0,2p)^2$ all pairs are shared ($b=a=0$, $s_2=s$): the maximum is $8c+6s=2(p-1)+2s$ with $1296^{c}6^{s}$
maximum sets.  Table~\ref{tab:shifts} shows the exact maxima for all boxes at $p=11$, $c=1$ (two generic orbits, $s=1$): the
values $22,24,26,28,30$ correspond to $(b,c)=(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)$ or $(0,1)$, $(1,0)$, $(0,0)$.  The number of boxes attaining
$3(p-1)$ depends on $c$ (for $p=13$: $28$ of the $169$ boxes for $c=1$, $4$ for $c=2$); it always includes the HJSW box
$x_0=-h$, $y_0\in\{0,1\}$.

\section{Beyond one hyperbola: what the theorem does not say}\label{sec:beyond}

Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:window} concern a single hyperbola.  Unions of two hyperbolae in the same box do
exceed $3(p-1)$, but --- as far as we can compute --- only by a few points.  For $\Pset_{k}=(H(1,p)\cup H(k,p))\cap\Gp$
($8(p-1)$ candidate points) exact maximum lawful subsets were computed in the project by four independent methods (SAT with
two cardinality encodings, kissat and CaDiCaL; mixed-integer programming, HiGHS; and constraint programming, CP-SAT; every
witness verified by an independent checker):

\begin{tabular}{rrrrrrp{6.8cm}}
\toprule
$p$ & $3(p-1)$ & $k$ & max & gain & LP relax. & method\\
\midrule
11 & 30 & 3 & 35 & $+5$ & 36.00 & MIP; all $k$: $32$--$35$ ($k=2$: 33, also SAT twice)\\
13 & 36 & 2 & 41 & $+5$ & 47.54 & SAT (two encodings), MIP; all $k$: $38$--$41$\\
17 & 48 & $-1$ & 54 & $+6$ & 60.15 & MIP; all $k$: $49$--$54$ ($k=2$: 53)\\

Three observations.  (i) The double count of Section~\ref{sec:proof} does not extend: rich lines of general slope
appear (a line meets each hyperbola in at most two classes, and copies of four different classes can be collinear), there
are $\Theta(p^2)$ of them, and the LP relaxation (``at most two per rich line'') is far from the truth (column ``LP''),
whereas for one hyperbola it is exact (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii)).  (ii) The gain over $3(p-1)$, maximised over $k$ ($+5,+5,+6,+5$ for $p=11,13,17,19$; all $k$ computed for $p\le17$,
$k\in\{-1,3\}$ for $p=19$; at $p=23$ the exact value is not settled, $70\le\max\le75$ for $k=-1$), shows no growth.  A parabola $y=x^2+k$ together
with $H(1)$ in the same box behaves alike: exact maxima $34,39,51,58$ for $p=11,13,17,19$ ($+4,+3,+3,+4$; MIP).
Three hyperbolae $H(1)\cup H(2)\cup H(3)$ ($12(p-1)$ candidates) give $37$ at $p=11$ (exact, MIP; $+7$) and at least
$42$ at $p=13$ (MIP, dual bound $47$; $+6$ so far) --- again a few points more than two hyperbolae, not a new order of growth.  (iii) A heuristic explanation: fix the HJSW set
$S_2$ of $H(1,p)$ and call a point $q$ of $H(k,p)\cap\Gp$ \emph{blocked} if some line through $q$ carries two points of
$S_2$.  The average number of blocking pairs per point grows like $\log p$ (about $3.1$ at $p=11$ and $7$ at $p=101$ for
$k\in\{2,3,-1\}$), and the number of unblocked points is $0$--$2$ for every $p\le101$ tested; freeing a point of the second
hyperbola costs deletions in the first.  This suggests that unions of hyperbolae in the $2p\times2p$ box gain $O(1)$ (or
$o(p)$) over the HJSW value, i.e.\ that their asymptotic ratio is still $3/2$; we have no proof.  The same picture appears
for hyperbolae with different moduli in one box: for $H(1,p)\cup H(1,q)$ in the $2p\times2p$ box (each hyperbola with its own
HJSW shift) the exact maxima (MIP) exceed $3(p-1)$ by $1$, $2$ or $3$ in all eleven pairs computed, $7\le p<q\le29$ ---
e.g.\ $32$ vs $30$ for $(11,13)$, $38$ vs $36$ for $(13,17)$, $50$ vs $48$ for $(17,19)$, $68$ vs $66$ for $(23,29)$ ---
and the number of collinear triples with points on both hyperbolae grows like $N\log N$, as for random sets of the same
density.

\section{Independent computer verification}

The theorem has a short self-contained proof; the following check is an independent sanity test of the bookkeeping
(the classification of the rich lines, the exceptional classes and the count of the maximum sets), performed by a
program that trusts no part of the proof.  The script \texttt{slack/hjsw\_window\_check.py} of the repository
recomputes, from the point set alone: all lines with at least three points of $\Pset$ (found by brute force over all
pairs of points; all have slope $\pm1$, three or four points, and lie inside one $V$-orbit); the identity
$2|\Lc|+Z=3(p-1)$; the exact maximum by exhaustive search inside each orbit (at most $2^{16}$ subsets), assembling a
maximum witness which is passed through the independent verifier \texttt{saturation.the\_law} in grid coordinates; and
the number of maximum sets, compared with $9^{s}$.  All checks pass for every prime $3\le p\le101$ (all $c\in\Fp^*$ for
$p\le31$; $c\in\{1,2,3,-1,-2,(p+1)/2\}$ for larger $p$); log: \texttt{slack/verification/hjsw\_window\_check\_all.log}.  For Theorem~\ref{thm:window} the script \texttt{slack/hjsw\_boxes\_check.py} (log \texttt{slack/verification/hjsw\_boxes\_check.log})
checks, for every box ($x_0,y_0\bmod p$) and every $c$: the formula $Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)$ against brute-force
line enumeration (random boxes, $p\le19$); $Z+2|\Lc|\le3(p-1)$ for all primes $p\le61$ (with equality for some boxes); the
orbit lemma orbit by orbit ($(e_O,f_O)\in\{(0,0),(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)\}$ only) for all $p\le31$; every identity used in the
proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit} ($\theta=c_a+c_b-k$, $\eta=c_b-c_a$, the four split conditions, parity, and
``$F_1\Rightarrow\neg(E_1\wedge E_2)$'') on random instances up to $p=199$; the maxima and multiplicities of
Lemma~\ref{lem:gadget} orbit by orbit (exhaustive search over the $2^{16}$ subsets) for all $p\le13$, all $c$ and all boxes;
and the formula $12a+10b+8c+6s$ against MIP optima on random boxes with $p\le31$.  The exact maxima of Table~\ref{tab:shifts}
are MIP optima (HiGHS) with witnesses through the law.  The values
agree with the exact maxima obtained earlier in the project by SAT solving (kissat/CaDiCaL, two encodings, witnesses
through the law): $18,30,36,48,54,66$ for $p=7,11,13,17,19,23$.  An external referee report on v0.1 of this note (kept
in \texttt{docs/reviews/}) re-derived the point sets and the rich lines with an independent implementation and confirmed
$3(p-1)$ and $9^s$ for all $c$ with $p\le31$ and for samples of $c$ with $p=37,43,59,73,101$.

script and its log were committed as \texttt{d422b78} (2026-08-17); the note itself is \texttt{paper/hjsw\_window.tex}.
