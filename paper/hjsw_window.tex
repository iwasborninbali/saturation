\begin{abstract}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $c\in\Fp^*$, and let $\Hc=\{(x,y)\in\Z^2: xy\equiv c \pmod p\}$ be the modular hyperbola.
Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild (1975) observed that the $2p\times2p$ window
$\Gp=[-\tfrac{p-1}{2},\tfrac{3p-1}{2}]\times[0,2p-1]$ contains exactly four points of each of the $p-1$ residue classes of
$\Hc$, at the vertices of a $p\times p$ square, and that keeping three of the four (the twelve outer blocks $T_2$ of the
$4\times4$ block subdivision of $\Gp$) yields $3(p-1)$ points with no three collinear.  Kov\'acs, Nagy and Szab\'o
(2025/26) state that this deletion is optimal, i.e.\ that $\Hc\cap T_2$ is a largest no-three-in-line subset of
$\Hc\cap\Gp$; the value $3(p-1)$ is therefore not new.  We give what is, to our knowledge, the first complete proof of
this statement, for every $c$, and we determine the complete extremal structure: we classify all lines carrying three or more points of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ (they have slope $\pm1$, and
their number is $\tfrac32(p-1)-s$), we exhibit an explicit linear-programming certificate for the bound $3(p-1)$
(so even the fractional relaxation has value $3(p-1)$), and we show that the number of maximum no-three-in-line
subsets is exactly $9^{s}$, where $s\in\{0,1,2\}$ is the number of quadratic residues among $c$ and $-c$.  In
particular the HJSW set is the unique maximum if and only if $p\equiv1\pmod 4$ and $c$ is a quadratic non-residue.
The bound does not depend on the position of the window: for \emph{every} $2p\times2p$ box $[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$
the modular hyperbola contains at most $3(p-1)$ points in general position, and we determine the exact maximum and the
number of maximum sets for every box (an ``orbit lemma'': a box separates $0$ or $2$ of the four partner pairs of each
generic orbit of the Klein four-group generated by $(a,b)\mapsto(-b,-a)$ and $(a,b)\mapsto(b,a)$, and the maximum is
$12n_{2}+10n_{1}+8n_{0}+6s$ in terms of the numbers $n_2,n_1,n_0$ of generic orbits with $2$, $1$, $0$ split pairs of each
kind); the HJSW box is among the boxes where
$3(p-1)$ is attained.  The classification also gives the no-four-in-line
analogue: the largest no-four-in-line subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ has exactly $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$ points, so the set $S_3$ of
Kov\'acs--Nagy--Szab\'o is optimal as well.  For the union of two hyperbolae $H(1)\cup H(-1)$ in the HJSW box we prove
$\alpha\le4(p-1)-4m_8(p)$ by an explicit weighted line cover, where $m_8(p)$ (the number of eight-point diagonals) is positive for all
$19\le p\le1500$ and equals $(1/12+o(1))\,p$ (by Bombieri's estimate along a cubic), so that
$\alpha\le(11/3+o(1))(p-1)$; to our knowledge this is the first bound below the trivial $4(p-1)$ for a union of two hyperbolae.
For an arbitrary second hyperbola $H(k)$ a ``potential'' cover along the cycles of the row/column incidence graph gives an exact
bound $\alpha\le4(p-1)-(2G_8-2\sum_c|A_c-B_c|)/R$ in terms of the number $G_8$ of eight-point lines and their distribution along the cycles,
and a character-sum estimate along the partner curve turns it into $\alpha\le4(p-1)-c\sqrt p/\log^5p$ for every $k$ of multiplicative order
at least $C\sqrt p\log^5p$, in particular for every primitive root.
The proofs are elementary and self-contained; independent programs check the statements about the HJSW box for all
$p\le101$ (all $c$ for $p\le31$), the bound $3(p-1)$ for all boxes with $p\le61$, and the box formula and orbit lemma
for all boxes with $p\le13$ and $p\le31$ respectively.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The no-three-in-line problem asks for the largest subset of the $n\times n$ grid with no three collinear points; at most
$2n$ points are possible, and the best general lower bound, $\tfrac32 n-o(n)$, is due to Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild
\cite{HJSW75}: for $n=2p$, $p$ prime, they place $3(p-1)$ points of a modular hyperbola $\Hc$ in the window $\Gp$.  Their
construction rests on two facts: every line meets $\Hc$ in at most two residue classes, and $\Gp$ contains exactly four
points of each class, at the vertices of a $p\times p$ square; keeping the three copies outside the middle block of the
$4\times4$ block subdivision of $\Gp$ leaves no three collinear.

Kov\'acs, Nagy and Szab\'o \cite{KNS25} recently used the same window and its refinements for the no-$(k+1)$-in-line
problem.  In their setting the HJSW set is the case $|W|=1$, $k=2$ of a general randomised construction (all four
copies of every class are taken, then a minimum set $R$ of points is deleted so that every line of slope $\pm1$ retains
at most $k$ points), and they state that for $|W|=1$, $k=2$ ``Subsection 2.2 essentially details an optimal choice for the points $R$ to be
removed to gain the largest no-3-in-line set $S_2(c,p)$ inside $S(W)$'' \cite[App.~A]{KNS25}.
The general estimate of \cite[Rem.~3.4]{KNS25}, $|R|\ge\tfrac12 X$ with $X=\sum_\ell(|\ell\cap S(W)|-k)^+$, gives for
$|W|=1$, $k=2$ the bound $|R|\ge p-1-s$ (see Remark~\ref{rem:kns} below), i.e.\ $|S|\le3(p-1)+s$ for every no-three-in-line
$S\subseteq\Hc\cap\Gp$: exact when neither $c$ nor $-c$ is a square modulo $p$, but one or two short of the truth
otherwise.  We are not aware of a proof of the exact statement in \cite{KNS25} or elsewhere; the purpose of this note is
to give one, and to determine the whole extremal structure of the problem.  To be explicit about what is new: the value
$3(p-1)$ and the qualitative optimality of $S_2$ and $S_3$ are asserted in \cite{KNS25}; the proof, the classification of
the rich lines, the LP certificate, the count $9^s$ with its uniqueness criterion, the exact value and count for every
$2p\times2p$ box, and the exact no-four-in-line value and count are, as far as we could determine, new.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Context.}  Modular hyperbolas $xy\equiv c\pmod m$ have a rich literature (see Shparlinski's survey
\cite{Shp12}; for combinatorial-geometric questions about them, e.g.\ \cite{KMSW}); the general problem of selecting a
largest subset in general position from a given point set is NP-hard and APX-hard \cite{FKNN17}, with general bounds in
\cite{PW13}, whereas here the structure of the point set makes the problem solvable exactly.  Variants of the
no-three-in-line problem with exact structural answers include the extensible version \cite{NNW23,Ghosal26} and the torus
version \cite{KW18}.  For $k\ge3$ points per line the asymptotic answer $kn$ was recently established
\cite{GGGKKP26}, while for $k=2$ the HJSW construction is still the best known general lower bound (see Green's
Problem 72 \cite{Green}); the state of the art for exact $2n$-point configurations is documented in Flammenkamp's
database \cite{FlammenkampDB} and in \cite{Prellberg26}.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Results.}  Throughout, $p$ is an odd prime, $h=(p-1)/2$, $c\in\Fp^*$, and
\[
 \Hc=\{(x,y)\in\Z^2:\ xy\equiv c \pmod p\},\qquad
 \Gp=\{-h,\dots,3h+1\}\times\{0,\dots,2p-1\},
\]
and $\Pset:=\Hc\cap\Gp$.  A set is \emph{lawful} (no-three-in-line, in general position) if it contains no three collinear points.  A line is
\emph{rich} if it contains at least three points of $\Pset$; $\Lc$ denotes the set of rich lines.  Let
$s=[\,c\text{ is a QR}\,]+[\,-c\text{ is a QR}\,]\in\{0,1,2\}$ (QR = quadratic residue modulo $p$).

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] Every rich line has slope $+1$ or $-1$; there are exactly $\tfrac32(p-1)-s$ rich lines, $p-1$ of them
 carrying three points of $\Pset$ and $\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ carrying four (Proposition~\ref{prop:lines} lists them); exactly
 $2s$ points of $\Pset$ lie on no rich line.
\item[(ii)] Every lawful subset of $\Pset$ has at most $3(p-1)$ points; the bound is attained.  Even the fractional
 relaxation (maximise $\sum_q x_q$ subject to $\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le2$ for $\ell\in\Lc$, $0\le x_q\le1$) has optimum
 $3(p-1)$.
\item[(iii)] The number of lawful subsets of size $3(p-1)$ is exactly $9^{s}$.  Every maximum set coincides with the
 HJSW set $\Hc\cap T_2$ outside the (at most four) classes $(a,\pm a)$ with $a^2=\pm c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:qr}
If $p\equiv3\pmod4$ then $s=1$ for every $c$: there are exactly $9$ maximum sets.  If $p\equiv1\pmod4$ then $s=2$ for
$c$ a QR ($81$ maximum sets) and $s=0$ for $c$ a non-residue: the HJSW set is the unique maximum.  Thus $\Hc\cap T_2$
is the unique largest lawful subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$ if and only if $p\equiv1\pmod4$ and $c$ is a quadratic non-residue.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:window}
Let $x_0,y_0\in\Z$ and $W=[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$.  Every lawful subset of $\Hc\cap W$ has at most $3(p-1)$
points.  More precisely, the maximum equals $12n_2+10n_1+8n_0+6s$, where $n_2,n_1,n_0$ are the numbers of generic
orbits of the Klein four-group whose partner pairs are split by the box in the patterns $\{(0,2),(2,0)\}$, $(1,1)$, $(0,0)$
respectively ($n_2+n_1+n_0=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$; no other pattern occurs), and the number of maximum sets is
$144^{n_1}\,1296^{n_0}\,9^{s_1}6^{s_2}$, where $s_1$ (resp.\ $s_2$) is the number of exceptional orbits whose pair is split
(resp.\ shared), $s_1+s_2=s$.  In particular $3(p-1)$ is attained iff $n_1=n_0=0$; the HJSW box has $n_2=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$,
$s_1=s$.  (Maximum sets are counted as subsets of $\Z^2$, not up to symmetry.  The pattern of an orbit and the split/shared
label of an exceptional pair depend only on the orbit and the box, not on the class chosen to name the orbit.)
\end{theorem}

The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a double count over the rich lines: for a lawful $S$, $|S|\le Z+2|\Lc|$ where $Z$ is
the number of points on no rich line, and the identity $Z+2|\Lc|=3(p-1)$ follows from the classification of the rich lines.  The count is
exact precisely because the $2s$ points that lie on no rich line are accounted separately; this is the correction to
the averaging bound of \cite{KNS25} mentioned above.  All rich lines lie inside orbits of a Klein four-group acting on
the classes, and the equality analysis reduces to two small ``gadgets'' (Figure~\ref{fig:gadgets}), which gives $9^s$.
For a general box the rich lines still lie inside the orbits, so the maximum is a sum of orbit maxima; the orbit lemma
(Section~\ref{sec:window}) says that a box splits $0$ or $2$ of the four partner pairs of a generic orbit, and the four
possible patterns have maxima $8$, $10$, $12$, $12$ --- whence the exact formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}.  For boxes
other than the HJSW box the maximum can be smaller than $3(p-1)$ (Table~\ref{tab:shifts}).
The four-point rich lines are pairwise disjoint, which settles the no-four-in-line analogue as well
(Corollary~\ref{cor:no4}: the maximum is $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$ in the HJSW box, attained by the set $S_3$ of \cite{KNS25}).
Section~\ref{sec:two} turns to unions of two hyperbolae: for $H(1)\cup H(-1)$ in the HJSW box, Theorem~\ref{thm:two} gives
$\alpha\le4(p-1)-4m_8(p)$ by an explicit line cover built from the eight-point diagonals and the two reflections of the box, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:m8asym} evaluates $m_8(p)=(1/12+o(1))p$; Corollary~\ref{cor:conics} shows that among all conics only the shifted
hyperbolae can reach $3(p-1)$ in a $2p\times2p$ box.  Section~\ref{sec:general} treats an arbitrary second hyperbola $H(k)$: an explicit
``potential'' cover along the cycles of the row/column incidence graph gives $\alpha(\Pset_k)\le4(p-1)-(2G_8-2\sum_c|A_c-B_c|)/R$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:general}), which is below the trivial bound whenever the eight-point lines are not too unevenly distributed along the cycles.  Section~\ref{sec:beyond} collects the computations that motivate the (unproved)
expectation that unions of hyperbolae gain only $O(1)$ over $3(p-1)$.

\section{Notation}

Two points are \emph{congruent} if their coordinates agree modulo $p$; a \emph{class} is a congruence class,
identified with an element of $\Fp\times\Fp$.  Points of $\Hc$ have both coordinates prime to $p$, so the classes of
$\Hc$ are the $p-1$ pairs $\kappa=(a,b)\in\Fp^*\times\Fp^*$ with $ab=c$.  Since each coordinate range of $\Gp$ is an
interval of $2p$ consecutive integers, it contains exactly two representatives of every residue class; hence
every class $\kappa$ of $\Hc$ has exactly four points in $\Gp$ and $|\Pset|=4(p-1)$.  We write
$(x_\kappa,y_\kappa)$ for the \emph{base copy} of $\kappa$, its unique point in
$\{-h,\dots,h\}\times\{1,\dots,p-1\}$ (necessarily $x_\kappa\ne0$), and
\[
 \kappa(r,s):=(x_\kappa+rp,\;y_\kappa+sp),\qquad r,s\in\{0,1\},
\]
for its four points in $\Gp$.  Following \cite{HJSW75,KNS25} we sort the classes into four \emph{types} according
to the position of the base copy:
\[
 A:\ x_\kappa>0,\ y_\kappa>h;\qquad B:\ x_\kappa<0,\ y_\kappa>h;\qquad C:\ x_\kappa>0,\ y_\kappa\le h;\qquad
 D:\ x_\kappa<0,\ y_\kappa\le h .
\]
(In the notation of \cite[Def.~2.3]{KNS25}: $\kappa(0,0)\in A_{0,0}$, $B_{-1,0}$, $C_{0,0}$, $D_{-1,0}$ respectively; the
HJSW set $T_2$ omits, for each type, the copy $A_{0,0}$, $B_{0,0}$, $C_{0,1}$, $D_{0,1}$, i.e.\ $\kappa(0,0)$ for
$A$, $\kappa(1,0)$ for $B$, $\kappa(0,1)$ for $C$, $\kappa(1,1)$ for $D$; the union of these omitted blocks is the middle
block $M$ of \cite[\S2.3]{KNS25}.  We call this copy the \emph{middle copy} of $\kappa$.)  We write $n_A,n_B,n_C,n_D$
for the numbers of classes of each type, so $n_A+n_B+n_C+n_D=p-1$.  Finally, for a class $\kappa$ put
\[
 d_\kappa:=x_\kappa-y_\kappa,\qquad e_\kappa:=x_\kappa+y_\kappa,\qquad
 D_\kappa:=\{x-y=d_\kappa\},\qquad E_\kappa:=\{x+y=e_\kappa+p\};
\]
$D_\kappa$ (the \emph{diagonal} of $\kappa$) contains $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ and $E_\kappa$ (the \emph{antidiagonal})
contains $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$.  For a real number $x$ let $x^+=\max\{x,0\}$.

Three involutions act on the classes of $\Hc$:
\[
 \sigma(a,b)=(-b,-a),\qquad \tau(a,b)=(b,a),\qquad \nu(a,b)=(-a,-b)=\sigma\tau(a,b)=\tau\sigma(a,b),
\]
so $V=\{1,\sigma,\tau,\nu\}$ is a Klein four-group acting on the $p-1$ classes.  $\sigma$ has fixed classes iff $-c$ is a
QR (then exactly two: $(a,-a)$ with $a^2=-c$), $\tau$ has fixed classes iff $c$ is a QR (two: $(a,a)$ with $a^2=c$),
and $\nu$ has none.  Put $f_\sigma:=\#\{\kappa:\sigma\kappa=\kappa\}\in\{0,2\}$, $f_\tau:=\#\{\kappa:\tau\kappa=\kappa\}\in\{0,2\}$;
thus $f_\sigma+f_\tau=2s$.  The $V$-orbits are of size~4 (\emph{generic}: four distinct classes) or of size~2
(\emph{exceptional}: $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ with $\kappa$ and $\nu\kappa$ both fixed by $\sigma$, or both fixed by $\tau$);
there are exactly $s$ exceptional orbits.

\section{Rich lines}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lagrange}
Let $\ell$ be a line containing at least two points of $\Hc$.  Then the points of $\Hc\cap\ell$ lie in at most two
classes.  More precisely, if $\ell$ contains a point of class $\kappa$, then the classes met by $\ell$ are:
only $\kappa$ if $\ell$ has slope $0$ or $\infty$; among $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ if $\ell$ has slope $+1$;
among $\{\kappa,\tau\kappa\}$ if $\ell$ has slope $-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $(u,v)\in\Z^2$ be a primitive direction vector of $\ell$ and $(x_0,y_0)\in\Hc\cap\ell$.  The lattice points of
$\ell$ are $(x_0+tu,y_0+tv)$, $t\in\Z$, and such a point lies on $\Hc$ iff
$uv\,t^2+(x_0v+y_0u)\,t\equiv0\pmod p$ (we used $x_0y_0\equiv c$).  The class of the point depends only on
$t\bmod p$.  If $uv\not\equiv0$, the congruence has at most two roots modulo $p$; if exactly one of $u,v$ is
divisible by $p$ (both cannot be, as $\gcd(u,v)=1$), it reduces to $x_0v\,t\equiv0$ or $y_0u\,t\equiv0$ with a unit
coefficient, so $t\equiv0$: one class.  For $(u,v)=(1,0),(0,1)$ this gives the claim for slopes $0,\infty$.
For $(u,v)=(1,1)$ the roots are $t\equiv0$ and $t\equiv-(x_0+y_0)$, the latter giving the point
$(-y_0,-x_0)$ modulo $p$, of class $\sigma\kappa$.  For $(u,v)=(1,-1)$ the roots are $t\equiv0$ and
$t\equiv y_0-x_0$, giving $(y_0,x_0)$, of class $\tau\kappa$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:copies}
Two distinct points of one class in $\Gp$ differ by $\pm(p,0)$, $\pm(0,p)$, $\pm(p,p)$ or $\pm(p,-p)$; the line
through them has slope $0,\infty,+1$ or $-1$ and contains no third point of that class.  The point $\kappa(r,s)$ lies
on the slope-$(+1)$ line $x-y=d_\kappa+(r-s)p$ and on the slope-$(-1)$ line $x+y=e_\kappa+(r+s)p$.  In particular
$D_\kappa$ and $E_\kappa$ are the only lines of slope $\pm1$ carrying two points of $\kappa$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The four points of a class are the vertices of an axis-parallel square of side $p$; no three are collinear.
The rest is a direct computation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:slopes}
A rich line has slope $+1$ or $-1$.  Consequently a set $S\subseteq\Pset$ is lawful iff $|S\cap\ell|\le2$ for every
$\ell\in\Lc$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange} three points of $\Pset$ on a line lie in at most two classes, so two of them are congruent,
and by Lemma~\ref{lem:copies} the line has slope $0,\infty,\pm1$.  A line of slope $0$ or $\infty$ meets a single class
(Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), which has at most two points on it (Lemma~\ref{lem:copies}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[position of the partner classes]\label{lem:partners}
For every class $\kappa$ the types of $\sigma\kappa$, $\tau\kappa$ and the shifts $d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa$,
$e_{\tau\kappa}-e_\kappa$ are given by the following table:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c|cc|cc}
\toprule
type of $\kappa$ & type of $\sigma\kappa$ & $d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa$ & type of $\tau\kappa$ & $e_{\tau\kappa}-e_\kappa$\\
\midrule
$A$ & $A$ & $0$ & $D$ & $-p$\\
$B$ & $C$ & $+p$ & $B$ & $0$\\
$C$ & $B$ & $-p$ & $C$ & $0$\\
$D$ & $D$ & $0$ & $A$ & $+p$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
In particular $\sigma$ preserves the types $A$, $D$ and interchanges $B$, $C$; $\tau$ preserves $B$, $C$ and
interchanges $A$, $D$; $\nu$ interchanges $A\leftrightarrow D$ and $B\leftrightarrow C$; the fixed classes of $\sigma$
lie in $A\cup D$, those of $\tau$ in $B\cup C$; and $n_A=n_D$, $n_B=n_C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\kappa=(a,b)$ with base copy $(x_\kappa,y_\kappa)$, so $x_\kappa\equiv a$, $y_\kappa\equiv b$.  The base copy of
$\sigma\kappa=(-b,-a)$ has first coordinate $-y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa\le h$ and $p-y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa>h$, and second
coordinate $p-x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa>0$ and $-x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa<0$ (these are the representatives of $-b$ in
$[-h,h]$ and of $-a$ in $[1,p-1]$).  Going through the four types:
$\kappa\in A$: $(p-y_\kappa,\,p-x_\kappa)$, again of type $A$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in D$: $(-y_\kappa,\,-x_\kappa)$, type $D$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in C$: $(-y_\kappa,\,p-x_\kappa)$, type $B$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa-p$;
$\kappa\in B$: $(p-y_\kappa,\,-x_\kappa)$, type $C$, difference $x_\kappa-y_\kappa+p$.
Similarly, the base copy of $\tau\kappa=(b,a)$ has first coordinate $y_\kappa$ if $y_\kappa\le h$ and $y_\kappa-p$
otherwise, and second coordinate $x_\kappa$ if $x_\kappa>0$ and $x_\kappa+p$ otherwise:
$\kappa\in B$: $(y_\kappa-p,\,x_\kappa+p)$, type $B$, sum $e_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in C$: $(y_\kappa,\,x_\kappa)$, type $C$, sum $e_\kappa$;
$\kappa\in A$: $(y_\kappa-p,\,x_\kappa)$, type $D$, sum $e_\kappa-p$;
$\kappa\in D$: $(y_\kappa,\,x_\kappa+p)$, type $A$, sum $e_\kappa+p$.
The remaining assertions follow.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:lines}
$\Lc$ consists of the following lines, and no others; for each we list its points in $\Pset$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For $\kappa\in A\cup D$ with $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$: the common diagonal $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$, with the
 four points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$.
\item[(b)] For $\kappa\in B\cup C$ with $\tau\kappa\ne\kappa$: the common antidiagonal $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, with the
 four points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(0,1),\tau\kappa(1,0)$.
\item[(c)] For every $\kappa\in C$: $D_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(1,0)$
 ($\sigma\kappa\in B$); for every $\kappa\in B$: $D_\kappa$ with the three points
 $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,1)$ ($\sigma\kappa\in C$).
\item[(d)] For every $\kappa\in A$: $E_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(1,1)$
 ($\tau\kappa\in D$); for every $\kappa\in D$: $E_\kappa$ with the three points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0),\tau\kappa(0,0)$
 ($\tau\kappa\in A$).
\end{enumerate}
The lines listed in (a)--(d) are pairwise distinct (apart from the identifications $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ in (a) and
$E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$ in (b) that are built into the list), and each of them lies inside a single $V$-orbit
of classes.  Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
 |\Lc|=\frac{n_A+n_D-f_\sigma}{2}+\frac{n_B+n_C-f_\tau}{2}+(n_A+n_D)+(n_B+n_C)
 =\frac32(p-1)-s,
\end{equation}
with $p-1$ three-point lines and $\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ four-point lines.  Moreover, the points of $\Pset$ lying on
\emph{no} rich line are exactly $\kappa(1,1)$ for $\sigma$-fixed $\kappa\in A$, $\kappa(0,0)$ for $\sigma$-fixed
$\kappa\in D$, $\kappa(0,1)$ for $\tau$-fixed $\kappa\in B$ and $\kappa(1,0)$ for $\tau$-fixed $\kappa\in C$; their number is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Z}
 Z:=\#\{q\in\Pset:\ q\text{ lies on no rich line}\}=f_\sigma+f_\tau=2s .
\end{equation}
The points lying on \emph{two} rich lines are exactly the middle copies of the classes $\kappa$ whose partner
($\sigma\kappa$ for $\kappa\in A\cup D$, $\tau\kappa$ for $\kappa\in B\cup C$) is different from $\kappa$; no point lies on three
rich lines.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\ell\in\Lc$.  By Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} its slope is $\pm1$; say $+1$ (the case $-1$ is symmetric, with
$\tau$, $e_\kappa$, $E_\kappa$ in place of $\sigma$, $d_\kappa$, $D_\kappa$).  By Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange} the points of
$\ell\cap\Pset$ lie in two classes $\kappa,\sigma\kappa$ (a single class carries at most two points on a line), and
one of the classes, say $\kappa$, has two points on $\ell$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:copies} these are $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$
and $\ell=D_\kappa$, and $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$.  The points of $\sigma\kappa$ on $D_\kappa$ are the
$\sigma\kappa(r,s)$ with $d_{\sigma\kappa}+(r-s)p=d_\kappa$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}: for $\kappa\in A\cup D$,
$r=s$, giving (a); for $\kappa\in C$, $r-s=1$, giving $\sigma\kappa(1,0)$; for $\kappa\in B$, $r-s=-1$, giving
$\sigma\kappa(0,1)$; this is (c).  Conversely each listed line has at least three points.

Distinctness: if $D_\kappa=D_\lambda$ then this line of slope $+1$ meets both classes $\kappa,\lambda$, so
$\lambda\in\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}; and $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ holds for $\kappa\in A\cup D$
(shift $0$) but not for $\kappa\in B\cup C$ (shift $\pm p$).  Likewise $E_\kappa=E_\lambda$ forces
$\lambda\in\{\kappa,\tau\kappa\}$, and $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$ holds exactly for $\kappa\in B\cup C$.  A rich line meets
only the classes $\kappa,\sigma\kappa$ (or $\kappa,\tau\kappa$), which lie in one $V$-orbit.  Counting: (a) gives
$(n_A+n_D-f_\sigma)/2$ lines, (b) $(n_B+n_C-f_\tau)/2$, (c) $n_B+n_C$, (d) $n_A+n_D$, which is \eqref{eq:L}.

For the incidences of a single point, take $\kappa\in A$.  Its points $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$ lie on $E_\kappa\in\Lc$
(d), and on no line of type (a)/(c) (their slope-$(+1)$ lines $x-y=d_\kappa\pm p$ carry no second point of
$\kappa$ and, by the table, no point of $\sigma\kappa$).  $\kappa(1,1)$ lies on $D_\kappa$, which is rich iff
$\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$, and on $x+y=e_\kappa+2p=e_{\tau\kappa}+3p$, which carries no point of $\tau\kappa$: so
$\kappa(1,1)$ is on one rich line if $\sigma\kappa\neq\kappa$ and on none otherwise.  $\kappa(0,0)$ lies on
$D_\kappa$ and on $x+y=e_\kappa=e_{\tau\kappa}+p$, i.e.\ on $E_{\tau\kappa}\in\Lc$: two rich lines if $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$,
one otherwise.  The types $D$, $B$, $C$ are analogous (for $B$: $\kappa(1,0)\in D_{\sigma\kappa}$ since
$d_\kappa+p=d_{\sigma\kappa}$, while $\kappa(0,1)$ lies on $x-y=d_\kappa-p=d_{\sigma\kappa}-2p$, which carries no point of
$\sigma\kappa$; for $C$ the roles of $(1,0),(0,1)$ are exchanged; for $D$: $\kappa(1,1)\in E_{\tau\kappa}$ since
$e_\kappa+2p=e_{\tau\kappa}+p$, and $\kappa(0,0)$ lies on $x+y=e_\kappa=e_{\tau\kappa}-p$).  This gives \eqref{eq:Z}
and the last two assertions.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:qr}}\label{sec:proof}

Part (i) is Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} and Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Upper bound.}
Let $S\subseteq\Pset$ be lawful.  For $q\in\Pset$ let $m(q)$ be the number of rich lines through $q$.  Every rich line
carries at most two points of $S$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}), hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:count}
\begin{aligned}
 |S| \;&=\; \#\{q\in S: m(q)=0\}+\#\{q\in S: m(q)\ge1\}
 \;\le\; Z+\sum_{q\in S}m(q)-\sum_{q\in S}\bigl(m(q)-1\bigr)^{+}\\
 &=\; Z+\sum_{\ell\in\Lc}|S\cap\ell|-\sum_{q\in S}\bigl(m(q)-1\bigr)^{+}
 \;\le\; Z+2|\Lc| ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
and by \eqref{eq:L}, \eqref{eq:Z}, $Z+2|\Lc|=2s+3(p-1)-2s=3(p-1)$.  The HJSW set attains the bound \cite{HJSW75}; the
equality analysis below exhibits all sets attaining it, so attainment does not depend on \cite{HJSW75}.

\smallskip\noindent\emph{LP certificate.}  The same count is valid for fractional $x\in[0,1]^{\Pset}$ with
$\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le2$ ($\ell\in\Lc$): $\sum_q x_q\le\sum_{q:\,m(q)=0}x_q+\sum_{\ell\in\Lc}\sum_{q\in\ell}x_q\le Z+2|\Lc|$.
In LP-duality terms the dual solution assigns weight $1$ to every rich line and weight $1$ to the bound $x_q\le1$ of each
of the $Z$ points on no rich line.  This proves (ii).  (The statement concerns the optimum value; we do not claim that the
polytope of the relaxation is integral.)

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Equality.}  $|S|=3(p-1)$ holds iff all inequalities in \eqref{eq:count} are equalities, i.e.\ iff
(I) $S$ contains all $Z$ points lying on no rich line; (II) $|S\cap\ell|=2$ for every $\ell\in\Lc$; (III) no point of $S$
lies on two rich lines.  Since every rich line lies inside one $V$-orbit of classes (Proposition~\ref{prop:lines}),
conditions (I)--(III) split into independent conditions on the orbits, and the number of maximum sets is the product of
the numbers of admissible choices in the orbits.  In a generic orbit $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa,\tau\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ all four
classes have distinct partners; the six rich lines meeting the orbit (two of type (a) or (b), four of type (c) or (d))
meet no other orbit, its four middle copies have $m=2$ and are excluded by (III), and each of the six lines then retains
exactly two points, both with $m=1$, which are forced into $S$ by (II): on a generic orbit $S$ coincides with
$\Hc\cap T_2$, and there is exactly one admissible choice.  An exceptional orbit $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ ($\kappa$ and
$\nu\kappa$ fixed by $\sigma$, or both fixed by $\tau$) meets exactly two rich lines, each with three points of $m=1$,
and contains two points with $m=0$: conditions (I)--(III) allow $\binom32\binom32=9$ choices, and all of them are lawful
by Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}.  There are $s$ exceptional orbits, whence $9^{s}$ maximum sets, all agreeing with the
HJSW set outside the exceptional orbits, i.e.\ outside the classes $(a,\pm a)$ with $a^2=\pm c$.  This proves (iii).

\smallskip\noindent\emph{Corollary~\ref{cor:qr}.}  $-1$ is a QR modulo $p$ iff $p\equiv1\pmod4$.  If $p\equiv3\pmod4$,
exactly one of $c,-c$ is a QR, so $s=1$; if $p\equiv1\pmod4$, $c$ and $-c$ have the same character, so $s\in\{0,2\}$
according as $c$ is a non-residue or a residue.  \qed

\begin{remark}\label{rem:kns}
(a) In the language of \cite[Constr.~3.2, Rem.~3.4]{KNS25} with $W=\{c\}$, $k=2$ (so $S(W)=\Pset$ and $L'$ is the set of
lines of slope $\pm1$): $X_{2,p}(\{c\})=\sum_{\ell}(|\ell\cap\Pset|-2)^+=(p-1)\cdot1+(\tfrac12(p-1)-s)\cdot2=2(p-1)-2s$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(i), so the general bound $|R|\ge\tfrac12X$ gives $|R|\ge p-1-s$ and hence, for every lawful
$S\subseteq\Pset$, $|S|\le|\Pset|-|R|\le3(p-1)+s$ (a lawful $S$ is a trimming, by Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes}).  The
averaging loses exactly one point per exceptional orbit, because each such orbit contains two points on no rich line
and no point on two rich lines; the count \eqref{eq:count} keeps track of these points and is exact.

(b) The theorem says that no lawful set can use the middle block $M$ profitably: adding fourth copies to the HJSW
set is impossible without removing at least as many points.  Any improvement of the HJSW construction must therefore
use points outside $\Hc\cap\Gp$ (another hyperbola, a larger window --- by Theorem~\ref{thm:window} not merely a
shifted $2p\times2p$ box --- or non-hyperbola points).

(c) The proof uses only: at most two classes per line (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), the square arrangement of the four
copies, and the position of the partner classes (Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}).  It does not use $p\ge7$; $p=3$ and $p=5$
are covered as well.
\end{remark}

\section{All $2p\times2p$ boxes}\label{sec:window}

Let $W=[x_0,x_0+2p)\times[y_0,y_0+2p)$ with $x_0,y_0\in\Z$ (only $x_0,y_0$ modulo $p$ matter), $\Pset_W=\Hc\cap W$, and let
$\rho_x(u)\in[x_0,x_0+p)$, $\rho_y(u)\in[y_0,y_0+p)$ denote the representatives of a residue $u$.  Every class $\kappa=(a,b)$
of $\Hc$ again has exactly four points $\kappa(r,s)=(\rho_x(a)+rp,\rho_y(b)+sp)$, $r,s\in\{0,1\}$, in $W$; put
$d_\kappa=\rho_x(a)-\rho_y(b)$, $e_\kappa=\rho_x(a)+\rho_y(b)$, $D_\kappa=\{x-y=d_\kappa\}$, $E_\kappa=\{x+y=e_\kappa+p\}$.
Lemmas~\ref{lem:lagrange}, \ref{lem:copies} and Corollary~\ref{cor:slopes} hold verbatim (they do not use the position of
the box), so a rich line is $D_\kappa$ for a class with $\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$ or $E_\kappa$ for a class with $\tau\kappa\ne\kappa$,
and its points are the two copies $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ (resp.\ $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$) together with the copies of the
partner class on the same line.  Since $d_\kappa\equiv d_{\sigma\kappa}\pmod p$ and both lie in an interval of length $2p-2$,
$d_{\sigma\kappa}-d_\kappa\in\{-p,0,p\}$; we call the $\sigma$-pair $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ \emph{shared} if
$d_{\sigma\kappa}=d_\kappa$ (then $D_\kappa=D_{\sigma\kappa}$ carries the four points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),
\sigma\kappa(1,1)$) and \emph{split} otherwise (then $D_\kappa$ carries $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$ and one copy of $\sigma\kappa$,
and $D_{\sigma\kappa}$ carries $\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$ and one copy of $\kappa$).  Likewise a $\tau$-pair is
shared ($E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, four points) or split (two lines with three points).  In the HJSW box the $\sigma$-pairs in
$A\cup D$ and the $\tau$-pairs in $B\cup C$ are shared and the others are split (Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}); in the box
$[0,2p)^2$ all pairs are shared.

\begin{lemma}[orbit lemma]\label{lem:orbit}
Let $O=\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa,\tau\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$ be a generic orbit, and let $e_O$ (resp.\ $f_O$) be the number of split
$\sigma$-pairs (resp.\ $\tau$-pairs) among the two $\sigma$-pairs and two $\tau$-pairs of $O$.  Then $e_O+f_O\in\{0,2\}$; in
particular $e_O+f_O\le2$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\kappa=(a,b)$, $X_u=\rho_x(u)$, $X'_u=\rho_x(-u)$, $Y_u=\rho_y(u)$, $Y'_u=\rho_y(-u)$ for $u\in\{a,b\}$.  The base copies
are $P=(X_a,Y_b)$ of $\kappa$, $Q=(X'_b,Y'_a)$ of $\sigma\kappa$, $R=(X_b,Y_a)$ of $\tau\kappa$ and $T=(X'_a,Y'_b)$ of $\nu\kappa$;
the $\sigma$-pairs are $\{P,Q\}$, $\{R,T\}$ and the $\tau$-pairs $\{P,R\}$, $\{Q,T\}$.  With $d(x,y)=x-y$, $e(x,y)=x+y$, the four
split conditions are $E_1: d_P\ne d_Q$, $E_2: d_T\ne d_R$, $F_1: e_P\ne e_R$, $F_2: e_Q\ne e_T$; each of the four differences is
$\equiv0\pmod p$ and lies in $[-p,p]$, hence is one of $-p,0,p$.

Let $m_x$ be the least multiple of $p$ exceeding $2x_0$ and $\mu_x=m_x-2x_0\in[1,p]$; similarly $m_y$, $\mu_y$.  Since
$X_u+X'_u\equiv0\pmod p$ and $X_u+X'_u\in[2x_0+1,2x_0+2p-3]$, we have $X_u+X'_u=m_x+\varepsilon_x(u)p$ with
$\varepsilon_x(u)\in\{0,1\}$, and explicitly $\varepsilon_x(u)=[X_u-x_0>\mu_x]$; likewise $Y_u+Y'_u=m_y+\varepsilon_y(u)p$,
$\varepsilon_y(u)=[Y_u-y_0>\mu_y]$.  Put $\Delta=\varepsilon_x(a)-\varepsilon_x(b)$, $\Gamma=\varepsilon_y(a)-\varepsilon_y(b)$,
$A=\varepsilon_x(b)-\varepsilon_y(a)$, $B=\varepsilon_x(a)-\varepsilon_y(b)$, and define the integers $\theta,\eta$ by
\[
 (X_a+X_b)-(Y_a+Y_b)-(m_x-m_y)=\theta p,\qquad (X_b-X_a)-(Y_b-Y_a)=\eta p .
\]
Substituting $X'_u=m_x+\varepsilon_x(u)p-X_u$, $Y'_u=m_y+\varepsilon_y(u)p-Y_u$ one finds
\[
 d_P-d_Q=(\theta-A)p,\quad d_T-d_R=(B-\theta)p,\quad e_R-e_P=\eta p,\quad e_Q-e_T=(\Gamma-\Delta-\eta)p,
\]
so that $E_1\Leftrightarrow\theta\ne A$, $E_2\Leftrightarrow\theta\ne B$, $F_1\Leftrightarrow\eta\ne0$,
$F_2\Leftrightarrow\eta\ne\Gamma-\Delta$, and $\theta-A,\ \theta-B,\ \eta,\ \eta-(\Gamma-\Delta)\in\{-1,0,1\}$.

\emph{Parity.}  For $x\in\{-1,0,1\}$, $[x\ne0]\equiv x\pmod2$.  Hence
$E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\equiv(\theta-A)+(\theta-B)+\eta+(\eta-\Gamma+\Delta)\equiv A+B+\Gamma-\Delta=2\varepsilon_x(b)-2\varepsilon_y(b)
\equiv0\pmod2$: the number of split pairs in $O$ is even.

\emph{If $F_1$ holds, then $E_1$ and $E_2$ do not both hold.}  Put $s=X_a-x_0$, $t=X_b-x_0\in[0,p-1]$ and $v=(x_0-y_0)\bmod p$;
since $Y_u\equiv X_u$, we have $Y_a-y_0=s+v-c_ap$ and $Y_b-y_0=t+v-c_bp$ with $c_a=[s+v\ge p]$, $c_b=[t+v\ge p]$, and therefore
$\eta=c_b-c_a$.  So $F_1$ means $c_a\ne c_b$; the substitution $a\leftrightarrow b$ exchanges $E_1\leftrightarrow E_2$ and
fixes $F_1$, so we may assume $c_a=0$, $c_b=1$, i.e.\ $s\le p-1-v<t$.  Since $\mu_x-\mu_y\equiv-2v\pmod p$ and
$|\mu_x-\mu_y|\le p-1$, we have $\mu_x-\mu_y=-2v+kp$ with $k\in\{0,1,2\}$, and
$\theta p=(2x_0+s+t)-(2y_0+s+t+2v-p)-(\mu_x+2x_0-\mu_y-2y_0)=p-2v-(\mu_x-\mu_y)=(1-k)p$, i.e.\ $\theta=1-k$.
With $\varepsilon_x(a)=[s>\mu_x]$, $\varepsilon_x(b)=[t>\mu_x]$, $\varepsilon_y(a)=[s+v>\mu_y]$, $\varepsilon_y(b)=[t+v-p>\mu_y]$
and $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v-kp$:
\begin{itemize}
\item[$k=1$:] $E_1\Leftrightarrow[t>\mu_x]\ne[s>\mu_x+v-p]$ and $E_2\Leftrightarrow[s>\mu_x]\ne[t>\mu_x+v]$.  If $s>\mu_x$
 then $t>\mu_x$ and $s>\mu_x+v-p$, so $E_1$ fails.  If $s\le\mu_x$, $E_2$ forces $t>\mu_x+v$, so $\mu_x+v\le p-2$ and
 $[t>\mu_x]=1$, and $E_1$ forces $s\le\mu_x+v-p<0$, impossible.
\item[$k=0$:] here $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v\le p$, so $\varepsilon_y(b)=0$ and $E_2\Leftrightarrow s\le\mu_x$; then $\varepsilon_y(a)=[s>\mu_x+v]=0$
 and $E_1\Leftrightarrow t\le\mu_x$, but $t\ge p-v>p-2v\ge\mu_x$.
\item[$k=2$:] here $\mu_y=\mu_x+2v-2p\ge1$, so $\mu_x\ge2p+1-2v$ and $\varepsilon_y(a)=1$; $E_1\Leftrightarrow t>\mu_x$ and
 $E_2\Leftrightarrow(s>\mu_x)\vee(t\le\mu_x+v-p)$.  Now $s>\mu_x$ would give $2p+2-2v\le s\le p-1-v$, i.e.\ $v\ge p+3$; so
 $s\le\mu_x$ and $E_2$ needs $t\le\mu_x+v-p<\mu_x$, contradicting $E_1$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, if $F_1$ holds then $E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\le3$, and if $F_1$ fails then again $E_1+E_2+F_1+F_2\le3$; by parity the
sum is $0$ or $2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[gadget maxima]\label{lem:gadget}
Let $O$ be an orbit and $\Pset_O\subseteq\Pset_W$ its $16$ (or $8$) points.  Every rich line meeting $\Pset_O$ lies in
$\Pset_O$, and the largest lawful subset of $\Pset_O$ has size and multiplicity as follows: exceptional orbit: $6$ points, $9$
sets if the pair is split and $6$ if it is shared; generic orbit with $(e_O,f_O)=(0,0)$: $8$ points, $6^4=1296$ sets;
$(1,1)$: $10$ points, $144$ sets; $(0,2)$ or $(2,0)$: $12$ points, one set.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A rich line meets only a class and its $\sigma$- or $\tau$-partner (Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}), hence lies in one orbit.
\emph{Exceptional orbit} $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa\}$, say $\sigma\kappa=\kappa$: the rich lines are $E_\kappa=E_{\nu\kappa}$ (four points)
if the $\tau$-pair is shared, resp.\ $E_\kappa$, $E_{\nu\kappa}$ (three points each) if it is split; the remaining four
(resp.\ two) points are free: $2+4=6$ points in $\binom42=6$ ways, resp.\ $2+2+2=6$ points in $3\cdot3=9$ ways.
\emph{Pattern $(0,0)$}: the two shared diagonals and the two shared antidiagonals are four disjoint four-point lines covering
$\Pset_O$: $4\cdot2=8$ points in $6^4$ ways.
\emph{Pattern $(2,0)$} (and symmetrically $(0,2)$): the four diagonals $D_\lambda$ ($\lambda\in O$) are rich with three points
each and the two antidiagonals $E_\kappa=E_{\tau\kappa}$, $E_{\sigma\kappa}=E_{\nu\kappa}$ have four points; every class $\lambda$ has
exactly one point of multiplicity $m=2$, namely its off-diagonal copy lying on $D_{\sigma\lambda}$ (which is also on the
antidiagonal of $\lambda$).  Each $D_\lambda$ carries $\lambda(0,0),\lambda(1,1)$ ($m=1$) and one $m=2$ point, each antidiagonal
carries two $m=1$ and two $m=2$ points.  By \eqref{eq:count} restricted to $O$ (with $Z=0$), $|S\cap\Pset_O|\le12$ with equality
iff $S$ avoids the four $m=2$ points and takes both remaining points of each of the six lines --- which is lawful; so the
maximum is $12$ and it is unique.  (For the HJSW box this is the generic orbit of Section~\ref{sec:proof}.)
\emph{Pattern $(1,1)$}: the split $\sigma$-pair and the split $\tau$-pair share exactly one class $\kappa$ (any $\sigma$-edge and
any $\tau$-edge of the four-cycle $\kappa\,\text{--}\,\tau\kappa\,\text{--}\,\nu\kappa\,\text{--}\,\sigma\kappa\,\text{--}\,\kappa$
meet in one vertex).  The six rich lines are
\begin{align*}
 A&:=D_\kappa,& B&:=E_\kappa,& A'&:=D_{\sigma\kappa},& B'&:=E_{\tau\kappa}&&(\text{three points each}),\\
 A''&:=D_{\tau\kappa}=D_{\nu\kappa},& B''&:=E_{\sigma\kappa}=E_{\nu\kappa}&&&&&&(\text{four points each}).
\end{align*}
Write $\kappa_A,\kappa'_A$ for the two copies of $\kappa$ on $A$ (these are $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1)$) and $\kappa_B,\kappa'_B$ for
the two on $B$; the third point of $A$ is a copy $\bar\sigma$ of $\sigma\kappa$ off its own diagonal, hence on $B''$; the third
point of $B$ is a copy $\bar\tau$ of $\tau\kappa$ off its own antidiagonal, hence on $A''$; the third point of $A'$ is a copy of
$\kappa$ off $A$, i.e.\ one of $\kappa_B,\kappa'_B$ --- name it $\kappa_B$; the third point of $B'$ is one of $\kappa_A,\kappa'_A$
--- name it $\kappa_A$.  No other incidences occur (a rich line of slope $+1$ through a copy of $\kappa$ is $A$ or $A'$, of
slope $-1$ it is $B$ or $B'$, and $A\cap B=\emptyset$ because a common lattice point would have $x=x_\kappa+p/2$).  Hence
$m=2$ exactly for the four points $\kappa_A\in A\cap B'$, $\kappa_B\in B\cap A'$, $\bar\sigma\in A\cap B''$, $\bar\tau\in B\cap A''$,
and $m=1$ for the other twelve: $A$ and $B$ consist of one $m=1$ point and two $m=2$ points, $A'$ and $B'$ of two $m=1$ points
and one $m=2$ point, $A''$ and $B''$ of three $m=1$ points and one $m=2$ point.
For lawful $S\subseteq\Pset_O$ let $t$ be the number of $m=2$ points in $S$; since every point of $\Pset_O$ lies on a rich
line, $|S|=\sum_{\ell}|S\cap\ell|-t$ over the six lines, each term being $\le2$.  If $t=0$ then $|S\cap A|,|S\cap B|\le1$
and $|S|\le1+1+2+2+2+2=10$; if $t=1$, the $m=2$ point lies on exactly one of $A,B$, the other of the two carries at most one
point of $S$, and $|S|\le11-1=10$; if $t\ge2$ then $|S|\le12-t\le10$.  So the maximum is at most $10$, and a set of size
$10$ makes every inequality used tight.  Counting these sets by $t$ (every set listed is lawful, since each rich line
receives at most two of its points):
\begin{itemize}
\item[$t=0$:] $S$ contains $\kappa'_A,\kappa'_B$, both $m=1$ points of $A'$ and both of $B'$ (forced), and two of the three
 $m=1$ points of $A''$ and of $B''$: $3\cdot3=9$ sets.
\item[$t=1$:] the $m=2$ point $q$ is one of $\kappa_A,\bar\sigma\in A$ or $\kappa_B,\bar\tau\in B$; say $q\in A$ (the case $q\in B$
 is symmetric).  Then $S\cap A=\{q,\kappa'_A\}$ and $S\cap B=\{\kappa'_B\}$; the second line through $q$ receives $q$ and one of
 its $m=1$ points, and each of the three remaining lines receives two of its $m=1$ points.  For $q=\kappa_A$: $B'$ in $2$ ways,
 $A'$ forced, $A''$ and $B''$ in $3\cdot3$ ways --- $18$ sets; for $q=\bar\sigma$: $B''$ in $3$ ways, $A'$ and $B'$ forced, $A''$
 in $3$ ways --- $9$ sets.  With the symmetric case $q\in B$: $2\cdot(18+9)=54$ sets.
\item[$t=2$:] the two $m=2$ points cannot both lie on $A$ (then $|S\cap B|\le1$ and $|S|\le11-2=9$), nor both on $B$; so one is
 on $A$ and one on $B$, every line receives exactly two points, and $S\cap A=\{q,\kappa'_A\}$, $S\cap B=\{q',\kappa'_B\}$.  For
 $\{q,q'\}=\{\kappa_A,\kappa_B\}$: $B'$ and $A'$ each receive their $m=2$ point and one of two $m=1$ points ($2\cdot2$), $A''$, $B''$
 two of three ($3\cdot3$): $36$ sets; for $\{\kappa_A,\bar\tau\}$: $B'$ $2$ ways, $A''$ ($\ni\bar\tau$) $3$ ways, $A'$ forced,
 $B''$ $3$ ways: $18$; for $\{\bar\sigma,\kappa_B\}$: symmetrically $18$; for $\{\bar\sigma,\bar\tau\}$: $B''$ $3$, $A''$ $3$,
 $A'$, $B'$ forced: $9$.  Total $36+18+18+9=81$.
\item[$t\ge3$:] $|S|\le12-t\le9$: no maximum set.
\end{itemize}
Altogether $9+54+81=144$ maximum sets of size $10$.  (An independent brute-force enumeration of the $16$-point gadgets,
\texttt{slack/gadget11\_check.py}, confirms the line structure and the counts $9,54,81$ for every $(1,1)$-orbit encountered
in all boxes for $p\le13$ and in samples of boxes for $p\le31$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:gadget} the rich lines of $\Pset_W$ lie inside the orbits, so a set is lawful iff its intersection with
every orbit is lawful, and the maximum and the number of maximum sets are the sum, resp.\ the product, of the orbit values.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit} every generic orbit has pattern $(0,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,2)$ or $(2,0)$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gadget}
gives the formula.  Since $n_2+n_1+n_0=\tfrac14(p-1-2s)$, the maximum is $12n_2+10n_1+8n_0+6s\le12(n_2+n_1+n_0)+6s=3(p-1)$,
with equality iff $n_1=n_0=0$.  For the HJSW box, Lemma~\ref{lem:partners} gives $(e,f)=(0,2)$ for the $A/D$-orbits and $(2,0)$ for the
$B/C$-orbits, and the exceptional pairs are split; this recovers Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(ii),(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[all conics]\label{cor:conics}
Let $C$ be a non-degenerate conic over $\Fp$ and $c\ge2$.  If the projective closure of $C$ contains both axial points at infinity
$(1{:}0{:}0)$ and $(0{:}1{:}0)$, then $C$ is a shifted modular hyperbola $(x+e)(y+d)=c'$, and for $c=2$ every lawful subset of its
lift into a $2p\times2p$ box has at most $3(p-1)$ points (Theorem~\ref{thm:window} after the shift).  Otherwise every lawful subset
of the lift of $C$ into a box of side $cp$ has at most $c(p+3)$ points; for $c=2$ and $p\ge11$ this is less than $3(p-1)$.  Hence
for $p\ge11$ no conic admits more than $3(p-1)$ lawful points in a $2p\times2p$ box, and only the shifted modular hyperbolae can
attain $3(p-1)$ (they do so exactly in the boxes described by Theorem~\ref{thm:window}, e.g.\ the HJSW box; a shifted hyperbola in
a box where $n_1+n_0>0$ attains less, and a conic that is $\Fp$-affinely equivalent to a hyperbola without being one, e.g.\
$(x-y)y=1$, is not covered by the shift and satisfies only the bound $c(p+3)$ --- for $p=7$ its lift into $G(7)$ has maximum $16<18$).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
A conic through $(1{:}0{:}0)$ and $(0{:}1{:}0)$ has no $x^2$ and no $y^2$ term, so its equation is $xy+dx+ey+f=0$, i.e.\
$(x+e)(y+d)=de-f$, a shifted hyperbola (non-degenerate iff $de\ne f$).  If, say, $(1{:}0{:}0)\notin C$, then $C$ has an $x^2$ term,
so every horizontal line $y=b$ meets $C$ in at most two points, and the number of residues $b$ with $C\cap\{y=b\}\ne\emptyset$ is
at most $\lceil|C(\Fp)|/2\rceil+1\le(p+3)/2$ (only the $\le2$ points with vertical tangent are alone on their line).  In a box of
side $cp$ the lift of $C$ therefore meets at most $c(p+3)/2$ rows, and a lawful set has at most two points per row.  The
symmetric argument applies if $(0{:}1{:}0)\notin C$.
\end{proof}
(A degenerate conic is a union of at most two lines, and the lift of an $\Fp$-line into a box of side $cp$ lies on $O(c\sqrt p)$
integer lines, so it contains $O(c\sqrt p)$ lawful points.  Note that $\Fp$-affine equivalence is \emph{not} a symmetry of the
lifted problem: only integer translations of the box are.  The observation that only the shifted hyperbolae contain both axial
points at infinity was made independently by reasoning agents consulted by the author of record, who also supplied the $p=7$
example.)

\begin{remark}[algorithmic consequence]
Selecting a largest subset in general position from an arbitrary point set is NP-hard \cite{FKNN17}; for
$\Hc\cap W$ Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:window} reduce it to bookkeeping: after listing the $p-1$ classes and their
$V$-orbits, the maximum, the number of maximum sets and an explicit maximum set are obtained in $O(p)$ operations (the
pattern of an orbit is read off from the split/shared status of its four pairs).
\end{remark}

The double count of Section~\ref{sec:proof} gives, for every box, $|S|\le Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)=12n_2+12n_1+8n_0+6s$
(one checks $Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+\#\{m=2\}$ and $\#\{m=2\}=2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)$ exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:proof}); it is
off by exactly $2$ on every $(1,1)$-orbit, where two rich three-point lines consist of one point of multiplicity one and two
of multiplicity two.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[no-four-in-line]\label{cor:no4}
In every box $W$ the four-point rich lines are pairwise disjoint; hence the largest subset of $\Pset_W$ with no four
collinear points has exactly $4(p-1)-N_{sh}(W)$ points, where $N_{sh}(W)$ denotes the number of shared pairs ($\sigma$-pairs
and $\tau$-pairs together; equivalently, the number of four-point rich lines of $\Pset_W$), and there are
$4^{N_{sh}(W)}$ such subsets.  By the orbit lemma $N_{sh}(W)\ge2\cdot\#\{\text{generic orbits}\}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$, so the
maximum is at most $\tfrac72(p-1)+s$; in the HJSW box $N_{sh}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ and the set $S_3=\Hc\cap T_3$ of
\cite[Constr.~2.10]{KNS25} (with $|S_3|=\tfrac72(p-1)+s$) is a largest no-four-in-line subset of $\Hc\cap\Gp$, as asserted in
\cite[App.~A]{KNS25}.  (No line carries five points of $\Pset_W$, so the no-five-in-line maximum is $|\Pset_W|=4(p-1)$.)
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
A four-point rich line is $D_\kappa$ for a shared $\sigma$-pair (points $\kappa(0,0),\kappa(1,1),\sigma\kappa(0,0),\sigma\kappa(1,1)$)
or $E_\kappa$ for a shared $\tau$-pair (points with $r+s=1$).  The slope-$(-1)$ line through a point $\kappa(r,r)$ is not of
the second kind: if it were $E_\lambda$ then $\kappa\in\{\lambda,\tau\lambda\}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:lagrange}, so $E_\lambda=E_\kappa$,
which contains only the copies $\kappa(0,1),\kappa(1,0)$ of $\kappa$; likewise the slope-$(+1)$ line through a point
$\kappa(r,1-r)$ is not of the first kind.  Hence the four-point lines are pairwise disjoint, a
no-four-in-line subset must miss at least one point of each of them, and deleting exactly one point from each is
sufficient (all other lines carry at most three points).  For the HJSW box, $N_{sh}=\tfrac12(p-1)-s$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}, and $|S_3|=|S_2|+|M\cap E\cap\Hc|=3(p-1)+\tfrac12(p-1)+s$ because the classes fixed by $\sigma$ or
$\tau$ lie in $E$ and the remaining classes split evenly between $E$ and $F$ \cite[Obs.~2.9]{KNS25}.
\end{proof}

For the box $[0,2p)^2$ all pairs are shared ($n_2=n_1=0$, $s_2=s$): the maximum is $8n_0+6s=2(p-1)+2s$ with $1296^{n_0}6^{s}$
maximum sets.  Table~\ref{tab:shifts} shows the exact maxima for all boxes at $p=11$, $c=1$ (two generic orbits, $s=1$): the

\section{Two hyperbolae: a first bound for $H(1)\cup H(-1)$}\label{sec:two}

Theorems~\ref{thm:main} and \ref{thm:window} concern one hyperbola.  For a union of two hyperbolae in the same box the exact maxima
are known only computationally (Section~\ref{sec:beyond}); the trivial bound is $4(p-1)$, because every column and every row of the
box that is not $\equiv0$ contains four candidate points (two lifts from each hyperbola) and a lawful set takes at most two of them.
Here we prove, for $k=-1$, a bound below the trivial one by an explicit weighted line cover.  Throughout this section
\[
 \Pset_{-1}:=\bigl(H(1,p)\cup H(-1,p)\bigr)\cap\Gp,\qquad |\Pset_{-1}|=8(p-1),
\]
and, for a slope-$(\pm1)$ integer line $\ell$, $|\ell|$ denotes the number of points of $\Pset_{-1}$ on $\ell$.  Let $m_8(p)$ be the
number of slope-$(+1)$ lines $x-y=d'$ with $|\ell|=8$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:two}
For every odd prime $p$, every lawful subset of $\Pset_{-1}$ has at most $4(p-1)-4\,m_8(p)$ points.
\end{theorem}

The proof rests on the two reflections of the box.  Put $R(x,y)=(p-x,\,y)$ and $R'(x,y)=(x,\,2p-y)$.  Both map $\Gp\cap\{xy\not\equiv0\}$
onto itself (the $x$-range $[-h,3h+1]$ is symmetric about $p/2$, the $y$-range $[1,2p-1]$ about $p$; the two omitted lines
$y=0$ and $x\in\{0,p\}$ carry no candidates), both map $H(1)$ onto $H(-1)$ and vice versa (since $(p-x)y\equiv-xy$ and
$x(2p-y)\equiv-xy$), and both interchange slope $+1$ and slope $-1$ lines: $R\{x-y=d'\}=\{x+y=p-d'\}$, $R'\{x-y=d'\}=\{x+y=2p+d'\}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rowsRR}
Every column $x\not\equiv0$ of the box contains exactly four points of $\Pset_{-1}$: the two lifts of the $H(1)$-class $\kappa=(x,1/x)$
and the two lifts of the $H(-1)$-class $R'(\kappa)=(x,-1/x)$.  Every row $y\not\equiv0$ contains exactly four points: the two lifts of
$\kappa=(1/y,y)\in H(1)$ and the two lifts of $R(\kappa)=(-1/y,y)\in H(-1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
On the column $x$ the candidates are the $y\in[0,2p)$ with $xy\equiv\pm1$: two lifts of $y\equiv1/x$ and two of $y\equiv-1/x$.  Rows
likewise.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[residue groups]\label{lem:groups}
Fix a residue $d\in\Fp$.  The points of $\Pset_{-1}$ on the slope-$(+1)$ lines $x-y\equiv d\pmod p$ are: the four lifts of each $H(1)$-class
$\kappa$ with $x_\kappa-y_\kappa\equiv d$ (at most two classes, $\kappa$ and $\sigma\kappa$), lying on the three lines $x-y=c_\kappa-p,\,c_\kappa,\,c_\kappa+p$
with $1,2,1$ points, where $c_\kappa=x_\kappa-y_\kappa$ is computed from the base copy; and the images under $R$ of the four lifts of each
$H(1)$-class $\lambda$ with $x_\lambda+y_\lambda\equiv-d$ (at most two classes, $\lambda$ and $\tau\lambda$), lying on the lines
$x-y=c'_\lambda-p,\,c'_\lambda,\,c'_\lambda+p$ with $1,2,1$ points, where $c'_\lambda=-(x_\lambda+y_\lambda)$.  All these centres lie in the
interval $[-h-p+1,\,h-1]$ of length $2p-2$, so a residue $d$ involves at most two distinct centres, $c$ and $c+p$.  In particular
$|\ell|\le8$ for every slope-$(\pm1)$ line, and $|\ell|=8$ for $\ell=\{x-y=c\}$ iff four distinct classes ($\kappa,\sigma\kappa,\lambda,\tau\lambda$;
none fixed by $\sigma$ resp.\ $\tau$) have the common centre $c$; then the sixteen points of these four classes lie on the three lines
$x-y=c-p,c,c+p$ with $4,8,4$ points, and no other point of $\Pset_{-1}$ lies on these three lines.  We call this a \emph{good residue group}
of slope $+1$; good residue groups of slope $-1$ are defined symmetrically (they are the images under $R$ of the good groups of slope
$+1$, so there are $m_8(p)$ of each slope).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The copies of $\kappa$ are $(x_\kappa+rp,y_\kappa+sp)$, on $x-y=c_\kappa+(r-s)p$: one point each for $(r,s)=(1,0),(0,1)$ and two for
$(0,0),(1,1)$.  A point of $H(-1)$ on $x-y=d'$ is $R(q)$ for a point $q$ of $H(1)$ on $x+y=p-d'$; the copies of $\lambda$ lie on
$x+y=x_\lambda+y_\lambda+(r+s)p$, whose $R$-images lie on $x-y=-(x_\lambda+y_\lambda)-(r+s)p+p$, i.e.\ centre $c'_\lambda$ with pattern $1,2,1$
(with $r+s=1$ on the centre line).  Since $x_\kappa\in[-h,h]$, $y_\kappa\in[1,p-1]$, both centres lie in $[-h-p+1,h-1]$.  A class fixed
by $\sigma$ (resp.\ $\tau$) is its own partner and contributes only its own two points to the centre line, so an $8$-point line needs four
distinct classes with a common centre; the count of points on $c\pm p$ is then $1+1+1+1$.  Only the classes with $x-y\equiv d$
(resp.\ $x+y\equiv-d$) have points on lines of residue $d$, so no other points occur.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:closure}
Let $\mathcal G$ be the set of all good residue groups of both slopes and let $B\subseteq\Pset_{-1}$ be the union of their points.
Then (i) two distinct good groups have no point in common; (ii) $R(B)=R'(B)=B$; (iii) every row and every column of the box is either
contained in $B$ or disjoint from $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) Two good groups of the same slope have different residues, hence disjoint point sets.  A good group of slope $+1$ contains, from
$H(1)$, a $\sigma$-pair $\{\kappa,\sigma\kappa\}$ with $c_\kappa=c_{\sigma\kappa}$ (a \emph{shared} pair, i.e.\ $x_\kappa$ and $x_{\sigma\kappa}=-y_\kappa\bmod p$
have opposite signs, types $A/D$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}), and from $H(-1)$ the $R$-images of a shared $\tau$-pair (types $B/C$).
A good group of slope $-1$ is the $R$-image of one of slope $+1$, so it contains from $H(1)$ a shared $\tau$-pair and from $H(-1)$ the
$R$-images of a shared $\sigma$-pair.  Since a class cannot be both $\sigma$-shared and $\tau$-shared (Lemma~\ref{lem:partners}: the former
means $\kappa\in A\cup D$, the latter $\kappa\in B\cup C$), an $H(1)$-class lies in at most one good group, and the same holds for
$H(-1)$-classes by applying $R$.  Hence the point sets are pairwise disjoint.
(ii) $R$ and $R'$ map $\Pset_{-1}$ onto itself and slope-$(+1)$ lines onto slope-$(-1)$ lines, so they permute the good groups.
(iii) By Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsRR} a row consists of the lifts of $\kappa$ and of $R(\kappa)$; the four lifts of a class are in $B$ or all
outside $B$ (good groups consist of whole classes), and $\kappa\subseteq B$ iff $R(\kappa)\subseteq B$ by (ii).  Columns: $\kappa$ and $R'(\kappa)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two}]
Let $S\subseteq\Pset_{-1}$ be lawful, so $|S\cap\ell|\le2$ for every line $\ell$.  If a family $\mathcal C$ of lines covers
$\Pset_{-1}$, then $|S|\le\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal C}|S\cap\ell|\le2|\mathcal C|$.  Take $\mathcal C$ = the three lines of every good residue
group (both slopes: $3\cdot2m_8$ lines) together with every row that is disjoint from $B$.  A point of $B$ lies on a line of its group
(Lemma~\ref{lem:groups}); a point outside $B$ lies on a row disjoint from $B$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:closure}(iii)); so $\mathcal C$ covers.
The rows disjoint from $B$ contain exactly the $8(p-1)-32m_8$ points outside $B$, four each (Lemma~\ref{lem:rowsRR}), so there are
$2(p-1)-8m_8$ of them, and $|S|\le2\,(6m_8+2(p-1)-8m_8)=4(p-1)-4m_8$.
\end{proof}
(The same count with weight $\tfrac12$ on the rows and on the columns disjoint from $B$ gives the same bound as a fractional cover.
Theorem~\ref{thm:two} was found independently, on the same day, by two of the agents mentioned in the disclosure; the point-by-point
verification of the cover for all $19\le p<320$ is \texttt{slack/block\_cover\_km1.py}, and for $p\le101$ \texttt{slack/km1\_theorem\_check.py}.)

\begin{proposition}[the number of eight-point lines]\label{prop:m8}
Let $X(u)\in[-h,h]$ denote the least absolute residue of $u\in\Fp^*$.  Then $4\,m_8(p)$ is the number of pairs $(a,b)\in(\Fp^*)^2$ with
\[
 ab^2+(a^2-1)b+a\equiv0\pmod p,\qquad a^2\not\equiv-1,\quad b^2\not\equiv1,\qquad X(a)X(1/a)<0,\quad X(b)X(1/b)>0,
\]
\[
 X(a)-X(1/a)+X(b)+X(1/b)\;=\;p\,\bigl([X(a)>0]-[X(b)<0]\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:groups} an eight-point line $x-y=c$ corresponds to a residue $d$ with an $H(1)$-class $\kappa=(a,1/a)$, $a-1/a\equiv d$,
whose $\sigma$-pair is shared and non-degenerate ($\sigma\kappa\ne\kappa$, i.e.\ $a^2\not\equiv-1$), and an $H(1)$-class $\lambda=(b,1/b)$,
$b+1/b\equiv-d$, whose $\tau$-pair is shared and non-degenerate ($b^2\not\equiv1$), with $c_\kappa=c'_\lambda$.  Eliminating $d$ from
$a-1/a\equiv-(b+1/b)$ gives the cubic.  Writing the base copies with $Y(u)=X(u)+p[X(u)<0]$: $c_\kappa=X(a)-Y(1/a)$ and
$c_{\sigma\kappa}=X(-1/a)-Y(-a)=X(a)-X(1/a)-p[X(a)>0]$, so the $\sigma$-pair is shared iff $X(a)$ and $X(1/a)$ have opposite signs, and then
$c_\kappa=X(a)-X(1/a)-p[X(a)>0]$; likewise $e_\lambda=X(b)+Y(1/b)$, $e_{\tau\lambda}=X(1/b)+Y(b)$, the $\tau$-pair is shared iff $X(b)$, $X(1/b)$
have the same sign, and then $c'_\lambda=-e_\lambda=-X(b)-X(1/b)-p[X(b)<0]$.  The equality $c_\kappa=c'_\lambda$ is the displayed equation.
Each line arises from exactly four pairs ($a\in\{a,-1/a\}$, $b\in\{b,1/b\}$).
\end{proof}
Since $a-1/a+b+1/b\equiv0$ on the cubic, the integer $L=X(a)-X(1/a)+X(b)+X(1/b)\in[-4h,4h]$ is a multiple of $p$, i.e.\ $L\in\{-p,0,p\}$;
the last condition selects one of these three values.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:m8asym}
$m_8(p)=\tfrac{1}{12}\,p+O(\sqrt p\,\log^4p)$.  Consequently $\alpha(\Pset_{-1})\le\tfrac{11}{3}(p-1)+O(\sqrt p\,\log^4p)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $C_0=\{(a,b)\in\Fp^2:\ ab^2+(a^2-1)b+a=0\}$.  Its only point with $a=0$ or $b=0$ is $(0,0)$.

\emph{Step 1: $C_0$ is absolutely irreducible and $|C_0(\Fp)|=p+O(\sqrt p)$.}  As a polynomial in $b$ over $K=\overline{\Fp}(a)$,
$F=ab^2+(a^2-1)b+a$ has discriminant $\Delta=(a^2-1)^2-4a^2=a^4-6a^2+1=(a^2-3)^2-8$, whose four roots $a^2=3\pm2\sqrt2$ are distinct
(their product is $1\ne0$ and $3\pm2\sqrt2$ are distinct, $p\ne2$), so $\Delta$ is not a square in $K$ and $F$ is irreducible over $K$;
being primitive in $b$ (its coefficients $a$, $a^2-1$, $a$ have no common factor), $F$ is irreducible in $\overline{\Fp}[a,b]$ (Gauss).
Hence $C_0$ is an absolutely irreducible plane cubic and, by the Weil bound for (possibly singular) absolutely irreducible curves,
$|C_0(\Fp)|=p+O(\sqrt p)$ (\cite[Ch.~11]{IK}; for singular curves \cite{AubryPerret}).

\emph{Step 2: the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:m8} are interval conditions on four linear forms.}  On $C_0$ we have
$1/b=-(a-1/a+b)$, so with $x_1=X(a)$, $x_2=X(1/a)$, $x_3=X(b)$ and $T=x_1-x_2+x_3\in(-3p/2,3p/2)$ the fourth least residue is
$X(1/b)=-T+p\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}$ chosen so that $|-T+p\varepsilon|\le h$, and $L=T+X(1/b)=p\varepsilon$.  The
conditions $X(a)X(1/a)<0$, $X(b)X(1/b)>0$, $L=p([x_1>0]-[x_3<0])$ therefore hold iff $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ lies in one of the four sets
\begin{align*}
 W_1&=\{x_1>0>x_2,\ x_3>0,\ p/2<T<p\},& W_2&=\{x_1>0>x_2,\ x_3<0,\ 0<T<p/2\},\\
 W_3&=\{x_1<0<x_2,\ x_3>0,\ -p/2<T<0\},& W_4&=\{x_1<0<x_2,\ x_3<0,\ -p<T<-p/2\}.
\end{align*}
Indeed, for $x_1>0>x_2$, $x_3>0$ we have $T\in(0,3p/2)$; for $T<p/2$ we get $\varepsilon=0$ and $X(1/b)=-T<0$ (excluded,
as $X(b)>0$), for $p/2<T<p$ we get $\varepsilon=1$, $X(1/b)=p-T>0$ and $L=p$, which is the required value $p([x_1>0]-[x_3<0])$,
and for $T>p$ again $X(1/b)=p-T<0$; this gives $W_1$, and the other three cases are analogous.  (So the condition on $L$ is implied by
the two sign conditions; it was kept in Proposition~\ref{prop:m8} because it is what the cover uses.)  Now pass to the torus
$\mathbb T^3=(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z)^3$ and to the points $\mathbf x=(a,a^{-1},b)/p \bmod 1$, one for each $(a,b)\in C_0(\Fp)$ with $a\ne0$;
there are $N=|C_0(\Fp)|-1=p+O(\sqrt p)$ of them.  With the four linear forms
\[
 \ell_1(\mathbf x)=x_1,\qquad \ell_2(\mathbf x)=x_2,\qquad \ell_3(\mathbf x)=x_3,\qquad \ell_4(\mathbf x)=x_1-x_2+x_3\pmod 1
\]
we have $X(u)>0\iff u/p\bmod1\in(0,\tfrac12)$ and $X(u)<0\iff u/p\bmod1\in(\tfrac12,1)$, and inside each octant the condition on $T$ is a
condition on $T/p\bmod1=\ell_4(\mathbf x)$: for $W_1$ ($T\in(0,3p/2)$) the condition $p/2<T<p$ is $\ell_4\in(\tfrac12,1)$, for $W_2$
($T\in(-p/2,p)$) the condition $0<T<p/2$ is $\ell_4\in(0,\tfrac12)$, for $W_3$ ($T\in(-p,p/2)$) it is $\ell_4\in(\tfrac12,1)$ and for $W_4$
($T\in(-3p/2,0)$) it is $\ell_4\in(0,\tfrac12)$.  Hence each $W_i$ is $\{\mathbf x:\ \ell_j(\mathbf x)\in I_{ij},\ j=1,\dots,4\}$ with
$I_{ij}\in\{(0,\tfrac12),(\tfrac12,1)\}$; the endpoints are never attained ($a,b\ne0$, $p$ odd, $T$ an integer), so the conditions
are exact.  Writing $x_i=\pm\tfrac p2U_i$ with $U_i$ uniform on $(0,1)$, the volume of $W_i$ is
$\tfrac18\Pr[1<U_1+U_2+U_3<2]=\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac23$ for $W_1,W_4$ and $\tfrac18\Pr[0<U_1+U_2-U_3<1]=\tfrac18\cdot\tfrac23$ for
$W_2,W_3$ (the density of $U_1+U_2+U_3$ has mass $\tfrac16$ on $[0,1]$ and on $[2,3]$; that of $U_1+U_2-U_3$ has mass $\tfrac16$ on
$[-1,0]$ and on $[1,2]$), in total $\sum_i\lambda(W_i)=\tfrac13$.

\emph{Step 3: exponential sums.}  For $\mathbf h=(h_1,h_2,h_3)\in\Z^3$ with $\mathbf h\not\equiv0\pmod p$ the rational function
$f_{\mathbf h}=h_1a+h_2a^{-1}+h_3b$ on $C_0$ is non-constant: if $h_3\not\equiv0$ a constant value would give $b\in K$, contradicting
the irreducibility of $F$ over $K$; if $h_3\equiv0$ then $h_1a+h_2/a=c$ for infinitely many $a$ forces $h_1\equiv h_2\equiv0$.  Its degree
is bounded independently of $\mathbf h$ and $p$, so it is not of the form $g^p-g+c$ for $p$ large, and Bombieri's estimate for
exponential sums along an absolutely irreducible curve \cite{Bombieri66} (see also \cite[Ch.~11]{IK}) gives
\[
 S(\mathbf h):=\sum_{\mathbf x}e(\mathbf h\cdot\mathbf x)=\sum_{(a,b)\in C_0(\Fp),\,a\ne0}e\Bigl(\frac{h_1a+h_2a^{-1}+h_3b}{p}\Bigr)=O(\sqrt p)
 \qquad(\mathbf h\not\equiv0\bmod p)
\]
with an absolute implied constant, while $S(\mathbf 0)=N$.

\emph{Step 4: counting with Selberg polynomials.}  Fix $H=\lfloor\sqrt p\rfloor$ (so $4H<p$ for $p\ge17$; the statement is asymptotic).  For an arc $I\subset\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$
there are trigonometric polynomials $S_I^-\le\mathbf 1_I\le S_I^+$ of degree at most $H$ (Selberg's polynomials, \cite[Ch.~1]{Montgomery};
\cite{Vaaler}) with $\widehat{S_I^\pm}(0)=|I|\pm\frac1{H+1}$, $|\widehat{S_I^\pm}(m)|\le\frac1{H+1}+\min\bigl(|I|,\frac1{\pi|m|}\bigr)$
for $0<|m|\le H$, and $0\le S_I^+\le C_0$ for an absolute constant $C_0$.  Fix $i$ and write $\mathbf 1_j=\mathbf 1_{I_{ij}}$,
$S_j^\pm=S^\pm_{I_{ij}}$, and $\mathbf n_j\in\Z^3$ for the coefficient vector of $\ell_j$ (so $\ell_j(\mathbf x)=\mathbf n_j\cdot\mathbf x$,
$\mathbf n_4=(1,-1,1)$).  The number of our points in $W_i$ is $\sum_{\mathbf x}\prod_{j=1}^4\mathbf 1_j(\ell_j\mathbf x)$.  By the
telescoping identity $\prod_jA_j-\prod_jB_j=\sum_j\bigl(\prod_{k<j}B_k\bigr)(A_j-B_j)\bigl(\prod_{k>j}A_k\bigr)$ and $0\le\mathbf 1_k\le1$,
$0\le S_k^+\le C_0$,
\[
 \Bigl|\prod_j\mathbf 1_j(\ell_j\mathbf x)-\prod_jS_j^+(\ell_j\mathbf x)\Bigr|\ \le\ C_0^3\sum_{j=1}^4\bigl(S_j^+-\mathbf 1_j\bigr)(\ell_j\mathbf x)
 \ \le\ C_0^3\sum_{j=1}^4\bigl(S_j^+-S_j^-\bigr)(\ell_j\mathbf x),
\]
and for each $j$, expanding $S_j^+-S_j^-$ in its Fourier series,
$\sum_{\mathbf x}(S_j^+-S_j^-)(\ell_j\mathbf x)=\sum_{|m|\le H}\bigl(\widehat{S_j^+}(m)-\widehat{S_j^-}(m)\bigr)S(m\mathbf n_j)
=\frac{2N}{H+1}+O\Bigl(\sqrt p\sum_{0<|m|\le H}\bigl(\tfrac1{H+1}+\tfrac1{|m|}\bigr)\Bigr)=O(\sqrt p\,\log p)$,
because $m\mathbf n_j\not\equiv0\pmod p$ for $0<|m|\le H$.  It remains to evaluate the smoothed count: with $\mathbf h(\mathbf m)=\sum_jm_j\mathbf n_j$,
\[
 \sum_{\mathbf x}\prod_{j=1}^4S_j^+(\ell_j\mathbf x)=\sum_{\mathbf m\in\Z^4,\ |m_j|\le H}\ \prod_{j=1}^4\widehat{S_j^+}(m_j)\ S\bigl(\mathbf h(\mathbf m)\bigr).
\]
The terms with $\mathbf h(\mathbf m)=\mathbf 0$ contribute $N\int_{\mathbb T^3}\prod_jS_j^+(\ell_j\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x$, and since each
$\ell_j:\mathbb T^3\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ is measure preserving ($\mathbf n_j$ is primitive), the same telescoping gives
$\bigl|\int\prod_jS_j^+(\ell_j\mathbf x)\,d\mathbf x-\lambda(W_i)\bigr|\le C_0^3\sum_j\int_0^1(S_j^+-\mathbf 1_j)=\frac{4C_0^3}{H+1}$.
The terms with $\mathbf h(\mathbf m)\ne\mathbf 0$ have $0<\|\mathbf h(\mathbf m)\|_\infty\le4H<p$, hence $|S(\mathbf h(\mathbf m))|=O(\sqrt p)$,
and their total is $O\bigl(\sqrt p\,(\sum_{|m|\le H}|\widehat{S^+}(m)|)^4\bigr)=O(\sqrt p\,\log^4p)$.  Altogether the number of our points in
$W_i$ is $N\lambda(W_i)+O(N/H+\sqrt p\log^4p)=N\lambda(W_i)+O(\sqrt p\,\log^4p)$.  Summing over $i$ and using $N=p+O(\sqrt p)$,
$\sum_i\lambda(W_i)=\tfrac13$: the number of pairs $(a,b)$ satisfying the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:m8}, up to the
$O(1)$ excluded pairs with $a^2\equiv-1$ or $b^2\equiv1$, is $\tfrac13p+O(\sqrt p\log^4p)$, so $m_8(p)=\tfrac1{12}p+O(\sqrt p\,\log^4p)$.
The last statement follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:two}.
\end{proof}
The data agree: $m_8(p)/p$ has mean $0.0825$ over the $193$ primes in $[200,1500]$ (range $0.058$--$0.108$; $1/12=0.0833$).

\begin{remark}
(a) The bound is attained at $p=11$: $m_8(11)=2$ and $\alpha(\Pset_{-1})=32=4\cdot10-8$ (exact maximum, Section~\ref{sec:beyond});
$m_8(13)=m_8(17)=0$ (no gain), and $m_8(p)\ge2$ for every prime $19\le p\le1500$ (computed), so the bound is below $4(p-1)$ from
$p=19$ on: for $k=-1$ the trivial bound $4(p-1)$ is never attained once an eight-point line exists.  In terms of the total number
$M_8=2m_8$ of eight-point lines of both slopes the bound reads $4(p-1)-2M_8$.  For $p=19,23,29,37,47$ it coincides with the best rank-1 (LP) certificate using rows, columns and slope-$(\pm1)$ lines,
which is $64,80,96,128,160$; for other $p$ that LP is somewhat smaller (it also exploits $5$--$7$-point lines with fractional weights).
(b) Proposition~\ref{prop:m8asym} makes the bound asymptotically explicit: $\alpha(\Pset_{-1})\le(11/3+o(1))(p-1)$, against the
trivial $4(p-1)$ and the computed maxima $3(p-1)+O(1)$ for $p\le23$.  The proposition quotes standard estimates (Bombieri's bound, Selberg's
polynomials) with unspecified absolute constants; the theorem itself is elementary and unconditional.  (Versions 1.0--1.1 of this note
had the error term $O(p^{5/6}\log^3p)$, obtained by covering the polytopes $W_i$ with boxes; the present $O(\sqrt p\log^4p)$ uses that
the $W_i$ are cut out by four linear forms, a simplification pointed out by an external referee-style report.)
(c) The argument uses that $R$ and $R'$ are symmetries of $\Pset_{-1}$; for a general second hyperbola $H(k)$ the rows pair a class of
$H(1)$ with a class of $H(k)$ that is not the image of the first under a symmetry.  Section~\ref{sec:general} replaces the block cover by a
``potential'' cover along the cycles of the row/column incidence graph and obtains an exact bound for every $k$; the case $k=-1$ is the one
where all cycles have length four, and Theorem~\ref{thm:two} is recovered.
\end{remark}


\section{Every second hyperbola: the potential cover}\label{sec:general}

Fix $k\in\Fp\setminus\{0,1\}$ and let $\Pset_k=(H(1)\cup H(k))\cap\Gp$.  Its $8(p-1)$ points fall into the classes
$\kappa_a=(a,1/a)\in H(1)$ and $\mu_b=(b,k/b)\in H(k)$, $a,b\in\Fp^*$, four points each (the two lifts of the $x$- and of the
$y$-coordinate).  Two elementary facts organise the box: every non-empty row $y$ carries exactly the four points of $\kappa_{1/y}$ and
$\mu_{k/y}$, and every non-empty column $x$ exactly those of $\kappa_x$ and $\mu_x$.  Hence, if we join two classes when they share a row
(``row-edge'') or a column (``column-edge''), every class has one edge of each kind and the \emph{class graph} is a disjoint union of even
cycles
\[
 \kappa_x\ \text{---}\ \mu_{kx}\ \text{---}\ \kappa_{kx}\ \text{---}\ \mu_{k^2x}\ \text{---}\ \cdots\ \text{---}\ \kappa_x ,
\]
one for each coset of $\langle k\rangle$ in $\Fp^*$, of length $2\operatorname{ord}(k)$, with the $\kappa$'s at the even and the $\mu$'s at
the odd positions.  A row-edge stands for two rows ($y$ and $y+p$), a column-edge for two columns; each of these lines carries two points of
each of its two classes.  Slope-$(\pm1)$ lines are organised in residue groups exactly as in Section~\ref{sec:two} (the residue $d$ groups the
classes with $a-1/a\equiv d$ and $b-k/b\equiv d$ for slope $+1$, and $a+1/a\equiv d$, $b+k/b\equiv d$ for slope $-1$); a group whose three
lines have $4,8,4$ points (an ``eight-group'') consists of four classes, two of each hyperbola, and each of its classes has two points on the
eight-point line and one point on each four-point line.  (Lemma~\ref{lem:groups} and its proof carry over with the obvious changes: the $H(k)$-classes of the slope-$(+1)$ group of residue
$d$ are $\mu_b,\mu_{-k/b}$ with $b-k/b\equiv d$, their two centres are equal or differ by exactly $p$, and they coincide iff $X(b)$ and
$X(k/b)$ have opposite signs; when both pairs of a group share their centres the two centres coincide automatically, for every $k$ ---
the sign bookkeeping of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:m8}; verified for all $p\le500$ and $k\in\{-1,2,3,5,7\}$, \texttt{slack/g8\_general.py}.)  Let
$G_8=G_8(k,p)$ be the number of eight-point lines of both slopes and $s(v)\in\{0,1,2\}$ the number of eight-groups containing the class $v$.
On a cycle $c$ put $A_c=\sum_{\kappa\in c}s(\kappa)$, $B_c=\sum_{\mu\in c}s(\mu)$ (so $\sum_cA_c=\sum_cB_c=2G_8$), and let $R_c$ be the range
(maximum minus minimum) of the walk $W_i=\sum_{j\le i}\varepsilon_js(v_j)$ along the cycle $v_0,v_1,\dots,v_{L-1}$, where $\varepsilon_j=+1$
at even and $-1$ at odd positions; $R=\max_cR_c$.

\begin{theorem}[every second hyperbola]\label{thm:general}
For every prime $p$ and every $k\in\Fp\setminus\{0,1\}$ with $G_8(k,p)\ge1$,
\[
 \alpha(\Pset_k)\ \le\ 4(p-1)\ -\ \frac{2G_8-2\sum_c|A_c-B_c|}{R}.
\]
For $k=-1$ all cycles have length four, $A_c=B_c$ and $R=1$, and the bound is $4(p-1)-2G_8=4(p-1)-4m_8$: Theorem~\ref{thm:two}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We exhibit a fractional cover: non-negative weights on lines such that every point receives total weight at least one; since a lawful set
meets every line in at most two points, its size is at most twice the total weight.  Put $t=1/R$ and weight $t$ on the three lines of every
eight-group (cost $6tG_8$; a point of a class $v$ receives $s(v)\,t$ from these lines).  On a cycle $v_0,\dots,v_{L-1}$ let $e_i$ be the
edge $\{v_i,v_{i+1}\}$ and give both lines of $e_i$ the weight
\[
 w_i=\tfrac12+(-1)^i\varphi_i,\qquad \varphi_i\in[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12].
\]
Every point of $v_i$ lies on one line of $e_{i-1}$ and one of $e_i$, hence receives $w_{i-1}+w_i=1+(-1)^i(\varphi_i-\varphi_{i-1})$ from rows
and columns; so the cover is feasible as soon as $\varphi_i-\varphi_{i-1}\ge-t\,s(v_i)$ at even $i$ and $\varphi_i-\varphi_{i-1}\le t\,s(v_i)$
at odd $i$ (the ``potential'' may drop by at most $ts$ at a $\kappa$ and rise by at most $ts$ at a $\mu$; moves in the other direction are
free), and $w\in[0,1]$ because $|\varphi|\le\frac12$.  The rows and columns of the cycle cost $4\sum_iw_i=2L+4\sum_i(-1)^i\varphi_i$, and for
even $L$ the alternating sum telescopes: $\sum_i(-1)^i\varphi_i=-\sum_{i\ \mathrm{odd}}(\varphi_i-\varphi_{i-1})$ (indices mod $L$).  Choose
$\varphi_i=\varphi_0-tW_i$ for $1\le i\le L-1$ (full moves at every special class); since $W$ ranges over an interval of length $R_c\le R=1/t$,
some $\varphi_0$ keeps $|\varphi|\le\frac12$ throughout.  The traversal ends at $\varphi_{L-1}=\varphi_0-t(A_c-B_c)$; close the cycle by one
free move at $v_0$ (a rise at the $\kappa$ $v_0$ if $A_c>B_c$, a drop at the $\mu$ $v_{L-1}$ if $B_c>A_c$; both endpoints are values of the
walk, so the move stays inside $[-\frac12,\frac12]$).  The sum of the odd-position jumps is then $tB_c-t\max(0,B_c-A_c)=t\min(A_c,B_c)$, and
the total cost is
\begin{align*}
 \sum_c\bigl(2L_c-4t\min(A_c,B_c)\bigr)+6tG_8&=4(p-1)-4t\Bigl(2G_8-\tfrac12\sum_c|A_c-B_c|\Bigr)+6tG_8\\
 &=4(p-1)-t\Bigl(2G_8-2\sum_c|A_c-B_c|\Bigr),
\end{align*}
using $\sum_cL_c=2(p-1)$ and $\min(A,B)=\frac12(A+B-|A-B|)$.
\end{proof}

The bound is exact for the cover with uniform line weight $1/R$: the class-level linear program with these line weights has exactly this
value for every $(p,k)$ we computed (\texttt{slack/exact\_c.py}, \texttt{slack/cyc\_model.py}), e.g.\ $\alpha(\Pset_3)\le3.914\,(p-1)$ at $p=199$
($G_8=34$, $R=4$) and $\alpha(\Pset_3)\le3.952\,(p-1)$ at $p=401$; when $\operatorname{ord}(k)$ is small the cycles are many and the
imbalance term can cancel the gain ($p=401$, $k=5$: sixteen cycles, $\sum_c|A_c-B_c|=G_8$, bound $4(p-1)$).

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:g8k}
For every $k\in\Fp\setminus\{0,1\}$, $G_8(k,p)=\tfrac16p+O(\sqrt p\log^4p)$ with an absolute implied constant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof (sketch)]
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:m8asym} goes through with the cubic $C_0(k):\ ab^2-(a^2-1)b-ka=0$ (from $b-k/b\equiv a-1/a$): its
discriminant in $b$, $a^4+(4k-2)a^2+1$, has the four distinct nonzero roots $a^2=1-2k\pm2\sqrt{k^2-k}$ when $k\notin\{0,1\}$, so $C_0(k)$ is
absolutely irreducible; an eight-point line of slope $+1$ corresponds to four points $(a,b)$ of $C_0(k)$ with $X(a)X(1/a)<0$ and
$X(b)X(k/b)<0$, and on $C_0(k)$ the fourth residue $X(k/b)$ is determined by $X(b)-X(a)+X(1/a)$ (substitute $b\mapsto-b$ in Step~2), so the
conditions are again interval conditions on the four linear forms $x_1,x_2,x_3,x_3-x_1+x_2$ of total volume $\frac13$; slope $-1$ is
symmetric.  All constants depend only on degrees.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[arc imbalance]\label{lem:arc}
There is an absolute constant $C$ such that for every prime $p$, every $k\in\Fp\setminus\{0,1\}$, every coset $a_0\langle k\rangle$ and every
arc $I=\{a_0k^j:\ J_1\le j\le J_2\}$ of the corresponding cycle,
\[
 \Bigl|\sum_{j=J_1}^{J_2}\bigl(s(\kappa_{a_0k^j})-s(\mu_{a_0k^{j+1}})\bigr)\Bigr|\ \le\ C\sqrt p\,\log^5p .
\]
Consequently $R\le2C\sqrt p\log^5p$ and $|A_c-B_c|\le C\sqrt p\log^5p$ for every cycle $c$; if $k$ is a primitive root, $A_c=B_c$ exactly.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(This argument is due to the first of the agents mentioned in the disclosure; its write-up with numerical checks is the file
\texttt{docs/research/}\allowbreak\texttt{arc\_imbalance\_lemma.md} of the repository.)
\emph{Fact 0.}  The own-pair conditions of the two slopes are complementary (for slope $+1$ the pair $\{a,-1/a\}$ shares its centre iff $X(a)$,
$X(1/a)$ have opposite signs, for slope $-1$ the pair $\{a,1/a\}$ shares iff they have equal signs; likewise for $\mu_b$ with $X(b),X(k/b)$), so
a class lies in an eight-group of at most one slope: $s(v)\in\{0,1\}$, $s=f^+_K+f^-_K$ on $H(1)$-classes and $s=f^+_M+f^-_M$ on $H(k)$-classes,
where $f^{\pm}_K(x)$ (resp.\ $f^\pm_M(y)$) is the indicator that $\kappa_x$ (resp.\ $\mu_y$) is a class of an eight-group of slope $\pm1$;
in particular $\sum_cA_c=\sum_cB_c=2G_8$.

\emph{Step 1 (complete character sums).}  For a nontrivial multiplicative character $\psi$ of $\Fp^*$ we show
$|\sum_xf^+_K(x)\psi(x)|\le C_1\sqrt p\log^4p$ (the three other functions are identical up to signs and the roles of $x,b$).  Now
$f^+_K(x)=1$ iff $x^2\ne-1$, the partner quadratic $b^2-(x-1/x)b-k=0$ has two roots (its discriminant $Q(x)=(x^4+(4k-2)x^2+1)/x^2$ is a
nonzero square), $X(x)X(1/x)<0$, and $X(b)X(k/b)<0$ for one (equivalently both) of the roots.  On the affine curve
$C_0(k):\ xb^2-(x^2-1)b-kx=0$ there are exactly two points over each such $x$, so
$\sum_xf^+_K(x)\psi(x)=\tfrac12\sum_{(x,b)\in C_0(k)(\Fp)}B_1(x)B_2(b)\psi(x)+O(1)$, where $B_1(x)$ is the indicator of
$X(x)X(1/x)<0$ and $B_2(b)$ that of $X(b)X(k/b)<0$.  Each of $B_1,B_2$ is a sum of two products of two interval indicators on $\Z/p$, and an interval
indicator has a Fourier expansion $\sum_m\hat c(m)e(mu/p)$ with $\sum_m|\hat c(m)|\le\log p+3$; hence
$\sum_xf^+_K\psi=\tfrac12\sum c^1_{m_1n_1}c^2_{m_2n_2}\,T(\psi;f)+O(1)$ with $\sum|c^1|,\sum|c^2|\le2(\log p+3)^2$ and
\[
 T(\psi;f)=\sum_{(x,b)\in C_0(k)(\Fp)}\psi(x)\,e\bigl(f(x,b)/p\bigr),\qquad f=m_1x+n_1x^{-1}+m_2b+n_2kb^{-1}.
\]
$C_0(k)$ is absolutely irreducible of genus one (its function field is $\Fp(x,\sqrt{Q_1(x)})$ with $Q_1=x^4+(4k-2)x^2+1$ squarefree for
$k\notin\{0,1\}$), the function $x$ has divisor $P_0^++P_0^--P_\infty^+-P_\infty^-$ with all multiplicities $\pm1$, so $x$ is not a $d$-th power
times a constant for any $d\ge2$ and the Kummer sheaf $\mathcal L_\psi(x)$ is geometrically nontrivial; the Artin--Schreier sheaf
$\mathcal L_e(f)$ has order $p$, coprime to the order of $\psi$, so $\mathcal L_\psi(x)\otimes\mathcal L_e(f)$ is nontrivial for \emph{every} $f$
(including $f=0$), and $f$ has at most four poles, all of order $\le1$.  By the Weil--Bombieri bound for mixed sums along curves
(\cite{Perelmuter}, \cite{Perret}; see also \cite[Ch.~11]{IK}) $|T(\psi;f)|\le C_2\sqrt p$ with $C_2$ absolute.  Summing, $|\sum_xf^+_K\psi|\le
2C_2\sqrt p(\log p+3)^4+O(1)$.

\emph{Step 2 (completion of the arc).}  Let $L=\operatorname{ord}(k)$, $r=(p-1)/L$, and $F(y)=s(\kappa_y)-s(\mu_{ky})$ (so the sum in the lemma is
$\sum_{j\in I}F(a_0k^j)$).  Expanding the indicator of $I\subset\Z/L$ in the characters $\chi_m(k^j)=e(mj/L)$ of $\langle k\rangle$ gives
$\bigl|\sum_{j\in I}F(a_0k^j)-\tfrac{|I|}{L}\hat F(0)\bigr|\le(\log L+1)\max_{m\ne0}|\hat F(m)|$ with $\hat F(m)=\sum_jF(a_0k^j)e(-mj/L)$; each
$\chi_m$ is the restriction of exactly $r$ characters $\psi$ of $\Fp^*$, and $\hat F(m)=\tfrac1r\sum_{\psi|\langle k\rangle=\chi_{-m}}\bar\psi(a_0)
\sum_yF(y)\psi(y)$, where for $m\ne0$ all these $\psi$ are nontrivial; by Step~1 (applied to $s(\kappa_y)$ and to $s(\mu_{ky})$, the latter being
$\bar\psi(k)$ times the sum for $s(\mu_y)$) $|\hat F(m)|\le4C_1\sqrt p\log^4p$.  Finally $\hat F(0)=\sum_{y\in a_0\langle k\rangle}F(y)=A_c-B_c$ is
$\tfrac1r\sum_{\psi|\langle k\rangle=1}\bar\psi(a_0)\sum_yF\psi$; the term $\psi=1$ is $(2G_8-2G_8)/r=0$ by Fact~0 and the others are again
$\le4C_1\sqrt p\log^4p$ (there are none if $r=1$).  Both bounds together give the lemma with $C=8C_1+O(1)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:general}
There are absolute constants $c,C>0$ such that for every prime $p$ and every $k\in\Fp\setminus\{0,1\}$ with $\operatorname{ord}(k)\ge C\sqrt p\log^5p$,
\[
 \alpha(\Pset_k)\ \le\ 4(p-1)-c\,\frac{\sqrt p}{\log^5p}\qquad(p\ge p_0).
\]
In particular this holds for every primitive root $k$, and for all but $O(p^{1/2+o(1)})$ values of $k$ (the elements of order $\le M$ in a
cyclic group of order $p-1$ number at most $\tau(p-1)\,M$).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:g8k}, $G_8\ge p/7$ for $p\ge p_0$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:arc}, $R\le2C\sqrt p\log^5p$ and
$\sum_c|A_c-B_c|\le\frac{p-1}{\operatorname{ord}(k)}\,C\sqrt p\log^5p\le G_8/2$ once $\operatorname{ord}(k)\ge14C\sqrt p\log^5p$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:general}
gives $\alpha(\Pset_k)\le4(p-1)-G_8/R\le4(p-1)-p/(14C\sqrt p\log^5p)$.
\end{proof}
The saving $\sqrt p/\log^5p$ is what the ``never blocking'' potential gives from the arc bound alone; numerically the potential cover with the
uniform weight $t=1/m$, $m=4,\dots,8$, saves a positive proportion of $G_8$ (Remark below), and Lemma~\ref{lem:arc} is numerically sharp
without the logarithms ($\max_\psi|\sum f_K\psi|/\sqrt p\in[1.2,2.7]$ for $p\le2003$, arcs $\le1.0\sqrt p$).

\begin{remark}
(a) The potential construction is exact in the following sense: for fixed line weights $t_G$ on the eight-groups, the cheapest assignment of
row and column weights is $2L-4D$ per cycle, where $D$ is the total deposit of a buffer of width one driven along the cycle, every $\kappa$
of an eight-group withdrawing at most $t_G$ and every $\mu$ depositing at most $t_G$; Theorem~\ref{thm:general} is the case of uniform
$t=1/R$, where the buffer never blocks.  (b) Numerically the uniform weight $t=1/m$ with $m=4,\dots,8$ is much better: it saves about
$0.2$ per eight-group on every $(p,k)$ computed ($p\le997$, $k=2,3,5$) and also on synthetic instances with randomly placed groups, i.e.\
$\alpha(\Pset_k)\le(4-c_0+o(1))(p-1)$ with $c_0\approx0.03$ appears to hold for every $k$; a proof needs the local statistics of the eight-group
classes along the cycles (a fixed-window equidistribution at the level of fibred products of $C_0(k)$), which we have not carried out.  The
LP with rows, columns and the eight-groups' lines saves about one per eight-group ($p\le601$), the ceiling of certificates of this kind.
\end{remark}

\section{Computational observations beyond one hyperbola}\label{sec:beyond}

Nothing in this section is a theorem: it records finite computations (all witnesses verified by an independent checker of all
triples; ``exact'' means that a solver proved optimality --- SAT: unsatisfiability at the next size, MIP/CP-SAT: zero gap --- and
``solver-proved'' upper bounds carry no independent certificate) and the picture they suggest.

\paragraph{Two hyperbolae in the HJSW box.}  For $\Pset_k=(H(1,p)\cup H(k,p))\cap\Gp$ ($8(p-1)$ candidates; trivial bound $4(p-1)$,

Observations.  (i) The gain over $3(p-1)$ is between $+1$ and $+6$ for \emph{every} $k$ at $p=11,13,17$ and shows no growth up to
$p=19$; the exact value at $p=23$ is not settled.  As an explicit falsifiable conjecture: $\alpha(\Pset_k)\le3(p-1)+6$ for all $p\ge11$
and all $k\notin\{0,1\}$ (consistent with all exact values; at $p=23$ the range $70$--$74$ still allows $+8$; a certified witness with
gain $\ge7$ would refute it).  (ii) The LP relaxation with all rich lines is far
from the truth (column ``LP''; $\approx4(p-1)-\varepsilon$), because for two hyperbolae the fractional $2$-factor $x\equiv\tfrac12$ is
almost feasible; certificates need lines with at least five candidates (Section~\ref{sec:two}).  Rank-$1$ certificates using only
rows, columns and slope-$(\pm1)$ lines give $\approx3.45\,(p-1)$ for $k=-1$ ($19\le p\le199$) and $3.6$--$3.9\,(p-1)$ for $k=2,3$
($p\le199$), and the integer optimum of that restricted system equals its LP value up to $2$ ($k=-1$, $p\le59$; \texttt{slack/verification/ip1\_km1.log}): so no local
inequality of any rank on those lines can certify less; the remaining loss down to $3(p-1)+O(1)$ is carried by lines of unboundedly
many slopes.  The anatomy of the rank-$1$ optimum for $k=-1$ is explicit: the slope-$(\pm1)$ lines with at least three points fall into
residue groups (all lines $x\mp y\equiv c\pmod p$) of four classes with line sizes $(4,8,4)$ [both the $\sigma$-pair of $\kappa$ and the
$\tau$-pair of $\lambda$ share their centres], $(3,7,5,1)$ [exactly one shares] or $(2,6,6,2)$ [neither], with densities $\to\tfrac1{12},
\tfrac1{12},\tfrac16$ per slope (each type is a polytope condition on $C_0$ as in Proposition~\ref{prop:m8asym}; \texttt{slack/km1\_lines\_5678.py}).
The optimum takes the three lines of every $(4,8,4)$ group (this is Theorem~\ref{thm:two}: saving $2$ per group), the lines $(7,5,3)$ of
the $(3,7,5,1)$ groups (locally certifiable saving $1$ per group and slope), and fractional weights on the six-point lines, whose saving
has no local certificate: forcing the $8$- and $7$-groups and optimising the rest reproduces the LP value for $p\le113$
(\texttt{slack/block\_cover2.py}, \texttt{slack/verification/lp1\_types\_p113\_137\_199.txt}).  A block lemma for the $(3,7,5,1)$ groups
would therefore improve Theorem~\ref{thm:two} to about $3.6\,(p-1)$; the LP constant $\approx3.45$ is not reachable by local
arguments, and neither constant is the truth.  (iii) Structure of the exact maxima: they are balanced between the two hyperbolae ($23+18$ at $p=13$, $30+29$ at
$p=19$), use one or two copies of almost every class, and the two copies of a class are usually in one column or one row (at $p=19$
the optimum consists of $23$ such vertical pairs and $13$ single copies; at $p=17$ some classes use three copies).  For $k=-1$ the
points fall into $(p-1)/2$ ``orbit blocks'' (the $16$ points of $\{\kappa,\nu\kappa,R\kappa,R'\kappa\}$ form a full $4\times4$ grid on
its rows and columns), the trivial bound is $8$ per block, and the optima have $\approx6$ per block on average with the deficits
spread over most blocks; pairs and triples of blocks essentially never conflict, so the loss is a global effect.  Restricted models
(only vertical/horizontal copy pairs; or $y$-periodic sets) reach $3(p-1)+O(1)$ but not the optimum.  

\paragraph{The vertical-pair model.}  For $k=-1$ consider the sets $S(r)$, $r\in\{0,1\}^{p-1}$, in which the class $\kappa_a=(a,1/a)$
contributes both lifts in the column $X(a)+r_ap$ and the class $(a,-1/a)$ both lifts in the other column $X(a)+(1-r_a)p$; every $S(r)$
has $4(p-1)$ points, two in every row and column.  Let $T(r)$ be the number of collinear triples of $S(r)$.  Three facts are proved:
(i) with $\varepsilon_a=(-1)^{r_a}$, $T$ is a quadratic form $T=E+\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}C\varepsilon$ ($S(1-r)=R'S(r)$ and $R'$ is a symmetry
of $\Pset_{-1}$, so the odd-degree Walsh coefficients vanish); here $8E$ is the number of collinear triples of $\Pset_{-1}$ with three
distinct classes, and $C$ is an explicit symmetric matrix with entries in $\tfrac18\mathbb Z$ and zero diagonal.  (ii) Every such collinear
triple is a sub-triple of a symmetric quadruple $q,\,q+s_1D,\,q+s_2D,\,q+(s_1+s_2)D$ ($D$ an integer direction, $uv\equiv2e/(s_1s_2)$,
$e=\pm1$) whose points lie alternately on the two hyperbolae, and the family $(s_1,s_2)$ occurs iff $s_1^2+s_2^2$ is a square modulo $p$
(the product $xy$ along a line is a quadratic in the parameter, symmetric about the midpoint of two equal-sign points).  (iii) Consequently
$E\ge c\,(p-1)\log\log p$ for all large $p$: for coprime $(s_1,s_2)$ with $s_1+s_2\le p^{1/4-\epsilon}$ and $\chi(s_1^2+s_2^2)=1$ the
Kloosterman count of directions $|u|,|v|\le p/(s_1+s_2)$ with $uv\equiv2e/(s_1s_2)$ gives $\gg p/(s_1+s_2)^2$ box-lifted triples, and
squares modulo $p$ are never absent from a dyadic scale of Gaussian norms (if all norms in $[M,2M)$ were non-residues, their pairwise
products, of norm in $[M^2,4M^2)$, would be residues, and the multiplicative energy of Gaussian integers, $\ll M^2\log M$, makes them
$\gg M^2/\log M$ in number).  Hence $\min_rT(r)\ge E+(p-1)\lambda_{\min}(C)$, and numerically this spectral bound is $\ge1.9\,(p-1)$ for
$11\le p\le19$ (true minima $24,28,42,48$; $80$ at $p=23$ and $136$ at $p=29$ against spectral bounds $64$ and $129$), $\ge3.6\,(p-1)$
for $41\le p\le71$, and $8.3,\,8.5,\,10.0,\,14.5,\,15.3\,(p-1)$ at $p=199,499,997,1999,4001$ ($E/(p-1)=17.2,\dots,28.1$;
\texttt{slack/vp\_lines.c}, \texttt{vp\_circulant.py}): every orientation carries many collinear triples, with a certificate that is not
a union bound.  What is missing for a
theorem is a Wigner-type edge estimate $\lambda_{\min}(C)\ge-K\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}C^2/(p-1)}$ (numerically $\lambda_{\min}=-(1.9\ldots2.1)\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}C^2/(p-1)}$ for all $p\le4001$
computed, $\sim-\sqrt{\log p}$, semicircle-like normalised moments; $\operatorname{tr}C^2/(p-1)=O(E/(p-1))$ is
proved), which would give $\min_rT\ge(1-o(1))E$; the trace method reduces it to a power-saving cancellation of signed sums over closed
chains of such quadruples --- an arithmetic statement we could not settle
(\texttt{docs/research/spectral\_programme.md}, \texttt{model\_theorem\_conditional.md}, \texttt{slack/vp\_*.py}).

\paragraph{Other unions.}  A parabola $y=x^2+k$ together with $H(1)$ in the same box: exact maxima $34,39,51,58$ for
$p=11,13,17,19$ ($+4,+3,+3,+4$; MIP).  Three hyperbolae $H(1)\cup H(2)\cup H(3)$ ($12(p-1)$ candidates): $37$ at $p=11$ (exact, MIP;
$+7$), between $43$ and $48$ at $p=13$ and between $56$ and $63$ at $p=17$ (CP-SAT 4 h, not closed).  Two hyperbolae with different
moduli, $H(1,p)\cup H(1,q)$ in the $2p\times2p$ box (each with its own HJSW shift): the exact maxima (MIP) exceed $3(p-1)$ by $1$, $2$ or
$3$ in all eleven pairs computed, $7\le p<q\le29$ (e.g.\ $32$ vs $30$ for $(11,13)$, $68$ vs $66$ for $(23,29)$), and the number of
collinear triples with points on both hyperbolae grows like $N\log N$ in the range computed ($N\le586$; ratio to $N\ln N$ near $11$),
as for random sets of the same density.  Larger boxes for one hyperbola: Remark~\ref{rem:larger}.

\paragraph{A heuristic.}  Fix the HJSW set $S_2$ of $H(1,p)$ and call a point $q$ of $H(k,p)\cap\Gp$ blocked if some line through $q$
carries two points of $S_2$.  The average number of blocking pairs per point grows like $\log p$ (about $3.1$ at $p=11$ and $7$ at
$p=101$ for $k\in\{2,3,-1\}$), and the number of unblocked points is $0$--$2$ for every $p\le101$ tested (for $k=2$ at $p=5$ and a few
other $(p,k)$ it is $4$); this depends on the chosen maximum set of $H(1)$ (over the $9^s$ maximum sets the number of unblocked points
varies between $0$ and $2$ at $p=13$, $k=2$) and does not lower-bound the deletion cost, but it explains why the second hyperbola
contributes little: freeing a point of $H(k)$ costs deletions in $H(1)$.  Whether the gain is $O(1)$, $O(\log p)$ or $O(\sqrt p)$
cannot be decided from the data ($+5,+5,+6,+5$); Theorem~\ref{thm:two} shows that for $k=-1$ the gain over $3(p-1)$ is at most
$(\tfrac23+o(1))(p-1)$, and no more is proved.  (Definitions and data: \texttt{slack/}, \texttt{docs/research/slack\_experiments.md},
\texttt{docs/research/pair\_bound\_notes.md}.)

\section{Independent computer verification}

The theorem has a short self-contained proof; the following check is an independent sanity test of the bookkeeping
(the classification of the rich lines, the exceptional classes and the count of the maximum sets), performed by a
program that trusts no part of the proof.  The script \texttt{slack/hjsw\_window\_check.py} of the repository
recomputes, from the point set alone: all lines with at least three points of $\Pset$ (found by brute force over all
pairs of points; all have slope $\pm1$, three or four points, and lie inside one $V$-orbit); the identity
$2|\Lc|+Z=3(p-1)$; the exact maximum by exhaustive search inside each orbit (at most $2^{16}$ subsets), assembling a
maximum witness which is passed through the independent verifier \texttt{saturation.the\_law} in grid coordinates; and
the number of maximum sets, compared with $9^{s}$.  All checks pass for every prime $3\le p\le101$ (all $c\in\Fp^*$ for
$p\le31$; $c\in\{1,2,3,-1,-2,(p+1)/2\}$ for larger $p$); log: \texttt{slack/verification/hjsw\_window\_check\_all.log}.  For Theorem~\ref{thm:window} the script \texttt{slack/hjsw\_boxes\_check.py} (log \texttt{slack/verification/hjsw\_boxes\_check.log})
checks, for every box ($x_0,y_0\bmod p$) and every $c$: the formula $Z+2|\Lc|=2(p-1)+2s+2\sum_O(e_O+f_O)$ against brute-force
line enumeration (random boxes, $p\le19$); $Z+2|\Lc|\le3(p-1)$ for all primes $p\le61$ (with equality for some boxes); the
